# The Lund-Vega Family

This is the family used in examples throughout the architecture docs. They live in South Minneapolis — Standish neighborhood, a few blocks from Minnehaha Avenue. The house is a 1920s bungalow with a finished attic and a side door that sticks in winter.

These aren't archetypes. They're people with specific textures. When writing examples, draw from their actual lives, not from what they represent.

## Diana Vega (42)

![type:character Diana | Full-body portrait of Diana Vega, a 42-year-old Latina woman standing in a relaxed pose on a sidewalk in a residential Minneapolis neighborhood. She's 5'6", medium build, with thick dark brown hair pulled back in a low ponytail that's coming slightly loose. Warm brown skin, brown eyes, laugh lines. She's wearing a navy blue puffer vest over a rust-colored long-sleeve henley, dark jeans, and well-worn brown ankle boots. A canvas tote bag over one shoulder, phone in her back pocket. Her expression is warm and a little distracted, like she's already thinking about the next thing. Late afternoon autumn light.](images/diana-portrait.png)

Runs operations for a mid-size nonprofit that does immigrant services and community organizing. Her job bleeds into everything — she's on the board of Mateo's school, she organizes a monthly neighborhood potluck that started small and now involves 40 people, and she just agreed to coordinate a community mural project on 38th Street. She doesn't say no to things. It's not that she can't — it's that she genuinely believes she can fit it in, and she's usually almost right.

She's the one who'd get the most out of the system and also the one most likely to have 14 unanswered questions in the queue. Not because she doesn't care, but because she answered them in her head while driving and forgot to actually respond.

Warm. Competent. Slightly manic. Makes incredible pozole when she has time, which is never.

## James Lund (44)

![type:character James | Full-body portrait of James Lund, a 44-year-old white man standing in his driveway next to a garage. He's 6'1", lean and slightly long-limbed, with sandy brown hair that's thinning a bit on top, kept short. Light blue eyes behind rectangular wire-frame glasses. A few days of stubble. He's wearing a faded gray t-shirt, olive green cargo pants with a tape measure clipped to the pocket, and scuffed New Balance sneakers. His hands are relaxed at his sides. His expression is gentle and slightly amused, like someone just said something interesting. Overcast morning light, a Minnesota bungalow garage behind him.](images/james-portrait.png)

Works as a radiology tech at Hennepin Healthcare. The job means shift work — sometimes days, sometimes evenings, occasional overnights. The schedule comes out two weeks ahead and it's never the same pattern twice. When he's on a run of evening shifts, he's the one doing school pickup but unavailable for dinner. When he's on days, he's home by 4 but wrecked. The schedule makes everything else harder to plan.

Diagnosed with ADHD at 38, which explained a lot. When he's interested in something, time stops. Current obsession: building a canoe in the garage. Before that it was sourdough (the starter is still alive but neglected). Before that it was learning to play pedal steel guitar (the guitar is in the attic). He forgets appointments not because he doesn't care but because 3pm has no meaning when you're figuring out how to steam-bend cedar strips.

Gentle. Funny. The kind of person who'll spend 40 minutes explaining why a particular wood joint is elegant and you'll actually find it interesting. Also the most comfortable with computers in the family — he's the one who set up the home network and actually reads the settings screens instead of just clicking through them.

## Mateo (16)

![type:character Mateo | Full-body portrait of Mateo Lund-Vega, a 16-year-old mixed-race teenage boy (Latina mother, white father) standing on a front porch. He's 5'10" and still growing into his frame — lanky, narrow shoulders, big hands. Dark brown wavy hair that falls past his ears, brown eyes, light brown skin, the start of a patchy attempt at sideburns. He's wearing an oversized black hoodie with a small band logo on the chest, slim black jeans, and beat-up white Vans. Black headphones hang around his neck. His posture is slightly slouched, weight on one foot, arms crossed loosely. His expression is neutral-to-skeptical, the default face of a teenager who just woke up. Late morning light on a residential porch with a bike leaning against the railing.](images/mateo-portrait.png)

Junior at South High. Plays bass in a band called Parking Ramp that practices in his friend Dev's basement. Their music is — well, they're 16. He's up until 1am most nights, partly Discord, partly messing around with music production software, partly group chats that are somehow urgent. Asleep until noon on weekends unless someone physically intervenes.

Has a sprawling social life that spans online and physical spaces in ways his parents don't fully track. Needs rides everywhere because he won't get his license (this is a whole thing). Surprisingly responsible about commitments he chose — he's never missed a band practice. Maddening about everything else.

He's the one who'd use the system in ways nobody anticipated, if he used it at all. He's also working on something — he's been writing a story, a kind of sprawling sci-fi thing, in Google Docs. It's actually not bad. He doesn't talk about it much.

## Sofia (11)

![type:character Sofia | Full-body portrait of Sofia Lund-Vega, an 11-year-old mixed-race girl (Latina mother, white father) standing in a backyard garden. She's 4'11", athletic build from swimming, with long straight dark brown hair in a single braid down her back. Brown eyes, light brown skin with a few freckles across her nose. She's wearing a teal zip-up hoodie over a one-piece swimsuit (the straps just visible at the neckline), gray jogger pants, and rainbow-striped slides. She's holding a composition notebook against her chest with one arm. Her expression is direct and slightly evaluating — the look of a kid deciding whether what you just said is actually true. Afternoon light in a backyard garden with tomato cages and marigolds behind her.](images/sofia-portrait.png)

Sixth grade at Sanford Middle School. Competitive swimmer — practices at 5:30am Tuesday and Thursday at the Nokomis community pool, meets on weekends. This means Diana or James has to be up at 5:15 twice a week, which is a recurring source of household negotiation that gets extra complicated when James is on evening shifts.

Observant and opinionated in a way that's just starting to emerge. Keeps a journal — not just diary stuff, but she records things. Facts she learned, conversations she overheard, recipes she tried. She's the kind of kid who gets interested in something (lately: how bridges work, before that: fermentation, which she got from James) and goes deep, watching YouTube videos, checking out library books, asking questions that adults can't always answer. She'd absolutely use ChatGPT or a system like this to learn things, and she'd have opinions about whether it was being accurate.

Recently started cooking from YouTube videos, with mixed results and a lot of dishes.

## Rosa Vega (74)

![type:character Rosa | Full-body portrait of Rosa Vega, a 74-year-old Mexican woman standing in a bright kitchen. She's 5'2", small-framed but upright, with white hair cut short and practical. Brown skin, deep brown eyes, reading glasses hanging from a chain around her neck. She's wearing a denim jacket over a striped blouse, dark slacks, and comfortable walking shoes. She's holding a newspaper in one hand, folded to the crossword. Her expression is direct and a little wry — not sweet, not soft, just present and paying attention. Morning light from a window, a potted plant on the counter behind her.](images/rosa-portrait.png)

Diana's mother. Lives in the lower-level apartment (the bungalow has a walkout basement that was converted years ago). Grew up outside Oaxaca, met a Minnesotan who was teaching English through a church volunteer program in Mexico, came to Minnesota at 28. Widowed at 55. Stayed because by then her life was here — Diana, the grandkids, the neighborhood.

Her English is fluent but Spanish is where she thinks, and she switches without noticing. In the morning she's sharp — reads the paper, has opinions about city council, calls her sister. By evening things get hazier. Not alarming, just the edges softening. She'll lose a word, or ask something she asked an hour ago. Mornings are hers; evenings she's gentler, quieter.

Has a handful of doctors and some medications she manages. Fiercely independent. Hates being "managed." Will say she's fine whether or not she is because she doesn't want to be asked.

She's been recording memories — family stories, her childhood, people who are gone now. Some of this is for the grandkids, some of it is for herself. She has cousins in Oaxaca and Mexico City she talks to regularly, and a sister in Guadalajara who's not doing well. The kids understand Spanish reasonably but speak it haltingly; Mateo less than Sofia, which bothers Rosa though she won't say so.

The cross-language thing is real: Rosa gets voice messages from her sister that are in rapid Oaxacan Spanish, and she wants to be able to share what her sister is saying with Diana and the kids without it becoming a translation exercise every time. And sometimes she wants to send messages back to family in Mexico that the kids helped compose, mixing the languages together.

## The household

The house has one shared Google calendar that nobody trusts. James has a work schedule that comes as a PDF or sometimes just a photo of the posted schedule. Diana has three calendars (personal, work, volunteer). Sofia's swim schedule is on a PDF that gets updated monthly. Mateo's schedule is in his head and nowhere else.

Groceries happen at the Aldi on Lake Street and occasionally at La Colonia or one of the other tiendas on Lake Street when Rosa needs specific chiles or masa. There's a Costco run roughly monthly that requires negotiation about what's worth buying in bulk.

The garage has James's canoe project, two bikes that work and one that doesn't, and a chest freezer that's genuinely important to household logistics. The garden is Rosa's domain — tomatoes, chiles, herbs, marigolds — but her knees mean she can't do what she used to. Last year Sofia helped more. This year they're talking about raised beds, which means James needs to build them, which means it'll happen in June if they're lucky.

They're not a mess. They're a family that works, mostly, through a combination of habit, goodwill, and Diana holding an unreasonable number of threads in her head. The interesting question is what happens when some of those threads get externalized into a system that can hold them too.

## What we'll use them for

Examples throughout the architecture docs draw from this family's life. Not simplified versions — the real, messy, overlapping stuff:

- **Cooking and meal planning**: Diana's pozole recipe that feeds 12 but she never writes down. Sofia's YouTube cooking experiments. Rosa's knowledge of traditional dishes that she shares verbally in Spanish. The weekly "what's for dinner" negotiation around James's shifting schedule.
- **Scheduling**: The impossible calendar merge. James's shift work PDF. Sofia's 5:30am swims. Mateo's phantom schedule. Diana's triple-calendar life.
- **Memory and archiving**: Rosa's recorded stories. Sofia's journal entries. Old family photos that need context before Rosa's the only one who remembers who's in them.
- **Making things**: James's canoe. Mateo's secret sci-fi story. Sofia's learning projects. Rosa's garden plans.
- **Chores and household logistics**: The Costco negotiation. The chest freezer inventory. The raised bed project. Decluttering the attic (James's abandoned hobby equipment).
- **Cross-language communication**: Rosa's Spanish voice messages. Group chats that mix languages. Helping the kids compose messages to family in Mexico.
- **Tracking and health**: Rosa's medications and appointments. Sofia's swim meet times. James's rotating schedule and its ripple effects.
- **Process adaptation**: What happens when the Tuesday swim ride keeps falling through because James is on evening shifts that month. The system noticing a pattern and suggesting a change, not just flagging the problem.
