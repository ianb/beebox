Two notes from the boxholder, minutes apart, recorded because they are design
seeds that are cheap to lose and hard to reconstruct. His words, exactly:

> chat history models the ai for the user, I'm trying to model the user for the
> ai

> between maximalist/implicit surveillance AI, and minimal/explicit (like
> chatgpt chat interface), I want bee box to be explicit but rich, where chat is
> explicit but poor

## What they say together

The second names two axes — richness and explicitness — and three corners are
already taken. Implicit and rich is the assistant that knows things you never
told it. Explicit and poor is the chat window, where you re-supply context every
session and it retains almost nothing. Implicit and poor is nobody's goal.
**Explicit and rich is the empty corner**, and it is the target.

The first says what would fill it. A transcript is an account of the
assistant's behavior, kept so a person can audit it: the AI is the subject and
the user is the reader. The inverse artifact — the user as subject, the agent as
reader — does not exist as a thing, though fragments accumulate across surfaces
whose primary jobs are something else: the boxholder person card, the
personality cards, the generated agent guide, the guidance and retrospective
procedures, the journals.

## Why this is not just "more history"

A transcript is append-only and can never be wrong, because it only claims
"this was said." A model of a person makes claims that go stale, get
contradicted, and need revision. That is a different contract, and it is why
accumulating more conversation does not produce one.

## The constraint that makes the corner reachable

A profile inferred into embeddings or a hidden memory store can be rich but
cannot be explicit: it can't be read, corrected, or have one wrong belief
deleted. Cards can be both, because the substrate is the thing the boxholder
already edits. That is an argument underneath
[interface as cards](../../beebox/docs/plans/interface-as-cards.md) that the
live workstream is approaching from the other direction.

It also sets the bar for any model-of-the-user work: it counts only if the
boxholder can see what the agent believes about him and change it. Something
that accretes silently is the implicit corner under a different name — and
speaks to the standing preference for arranging context over automating
judgment.

## Open

- What the artifact is: one card, a landmark, a derived view over existing
  material?
- Who maintains it, and how a stale or contradicted claim gets revised.
- What an agent reads before a turn, versus what a person reads when they want
  to know what the box thinks of them.
- Whether "explicit" requires authorship (the boxholder wrote it) or only
  visibility (the agent wrote it, he can read and edit it).
