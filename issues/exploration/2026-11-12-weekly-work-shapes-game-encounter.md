The boxholder brought up a simple 2D game concept: jobs completed during a
week add allies, and jobs missed add foes. The appeal is both seeing the week
transformed into a game world and feeling the tactical effect during play.
The person plays the resulting game. This is an idea to explore, not an
adopted mechanic or a request to build it now.

This could be one way for the outer game in the [game framework
discussion](2026-10-26-box-game-framework-plugin.md) to turn real activity
into a playable encounter. The relationship is still open: this game might
be one mini-game, or its changing cast might be part of the outer loop.

## Open questions

- Which jobs count, and who decides whether a job was missed? How are
  partial progress, changed plans, and a difficult week represented?
- Does one encounter assemble at the end of the week, or does the game
  change as jobs are completed or missed?
- What do allies and foes do in play? How can both the week's meaning and
  the tactical consequences remain visible without making the encounter
  unplayable after many missed jobs?
- How does a game victory relate to an unfinished real job? Defeating a foe
  should not silently mark that job complete.
- How can the player choose or revise these terms without turning the game
  into an unrequested guilt or streak mechanic?
