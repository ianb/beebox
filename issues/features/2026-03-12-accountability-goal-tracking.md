Someone sets a personal goal — like drinking water 3 times a day, or practicing guitar — and messages the box when they do it. The box tracks check-ins throughout the day, then posts a summary to the group chat at the end of the day: did they hit their target or not? The family provides the accountability. The goal is a card, check-ins come through chat, and the end-of-day report is a scheduled procedure. Could be playful — streaks, encouragement from the box, family members commenting.

## Research (2026-10-08)

Two mechanisms worth taking from Edi Life OS
([review](../../research/lifeos-review.md)), a self-hosted habit/goal app:

- **Progress derived from check-ins, never copied.** A goal names its
  check-in source and a target count over a start/deadline window. Progress
  is a query over dated check-in records (count of distinct days), so undoing
  a check-in lowers it and the goal card stores no running total. In Bee Box
  terms: the goal card carries the source and target; check-ins are dated
  records written from chat; a view or the end-of-day procedure computes the
  count.
- **The review carries last period's next step forward.** Their weekly
  review shows the previous "next step" beside the current indicators and
  freezes a numeric snapshot at save time so history does not drift. For Bee
  Box this is a scheduled procedure that opens a conversation with those two
  facts in front of the person, not a form.

A full "life OS" (dimensions, dashboards, scores) is rejected in the review;
this family-accountability variant is the part that fits the box.
