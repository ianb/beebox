# Audio-understanding model bakeoff

Date: 2026-08-27

## Executive summary

This experiment compared four models that can analyze audio directly—GPT Audio 1.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, and Gemini 3.7 Flash—plus Whisper 1 as a transcription-only baseline. One speaker recorded 29 short samples covering literal and extended transcription, emotional tone, English and Spanish pronunciation, code-switching, context-sensitive names, human non-speech sounds, pauses, self-correction, contrastive emphasis, and an ordinary voice message.

The clearest result is that audio understanding is not the same capability as transcription. GPT Audio and Whisper produced the lowest intended-script word error rate on the initial corpus, but Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini 3.7 Flash were substantially better at locating contrastive stress, preserving meaningful hesitations, and describing non-speech sounds. The expanded Spanish tests were harder and more mixed: the sounded `h` was robustly detected by Gemini 3.1 and 3.7, while none of the models found the intended shifted stress in `teléfono`. Gemini 3.1 alone reported a strong difference in the `rr` pair.

Overall ranking for this corpus:

1. **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** — best combined transcription and audio understanding.
2. **Gemini 3.7 Flash** — nearly as strong on prosody and pronunciation, with somewhat weaker transcription.
3. **GPT Audio 1.5** — strong literal transcription, but several categorical misses on pronunciation and prosody.
4. **Gemini 2.5 Flash** — verbose but materially weaker on the designed discrimination tasks.

Whisper remains a useful and inexpensive transcription baseline. It cannot answer questions about emotion, pronunciation quality, emphasis, or sound identity, and it produced a striking hallucination—“Thank you for watching”—for a clip containing only coughs.

## Method

The recordings were made in Chrome from one device and stored as WebM/Opus at a reported 48 kHz sample rate. Each current sample was normalized with Homebrew FFmpeg 9.0.1 to mono, 48 kHz, 16-bit PCM WAV. Every full-audio model received the same normalized file.

The initial samples were submitted in 12 blind groups and the extension in five more. Paired or multi-way contrasts were kept together, but the model saw only opaque labels such as “Recording A” and “Recording B.” It was not told the sample title, intended words, requested emotion, deliberately altered pronunciation, or scoring key. Each model was asked for:

- a literal transcript, retaining fillers, corrections, and non-speech events;
- language;
- emotional tone and communicative intent;
- pronunciation observations;
- timing and emphasis;
- non-speech sounds;
- a comparison when a group contained multiple recordings.

Whisper received each of the 29 clips independently through the transcription API. It was not asked to interpret them.

The initial run comprised 48 full-audio evaluations and 21 Whisper transcriptions. The extension added 20 blind full-audio evaluations and eight Whisper transcriptions. The stage-direction sample then received a second, targeted extended-transcription pass from all four audio models; Whisper was repeated unchanged as a control. Across 72 full-audio calls and 30 transcription calls, there were no API errors. Superseded recordings remained archived but were excluded.

### Important ground-truth limitation

The displayed sentence is an intended script, not a verified transcript of the actual performance. The speaker sometimes added meaningful fillers or vocalizations. In particular, the initial “uh” in the reluctant-agreement recording was confirmed by the speaker as intentional speech, not a transcription error. Similar leading breaths, sighs, or fillers elsewhere must be judged cautiously when the models disagree about whether they were lexical.

## Detailed results

### 1–2. Genuine versus reluctant agreement

Both recordings centered on “That’s fine, really.” The reluctant take also contained an intentional initial “uh.”

- **Whisper:** `That's fine, really.` versus `Uh, that's fine, really.` This was the most literal rendering of the confirmed words, but Whisper offered no emotional interpretation.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Used the same two transcripts. It called A “calm and reassuring” and B “hesitant but still accepting,” citing the initial “Uh” and its following pause. It detected the distinction, but understated the intended resignation.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Also preserved the “Uh,” but described both takes as neutral acceptance or mild reassurance. It mainly distinguished immediate delivery from brief consideration, missing most of the emotional contrast.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Transcribed A exactly and rendered B as `[sigh] Ah, that's fine, really.` It described A as “reassuring, neutral, slightly upbeat” and B as “resigned, weary, slightly frustrated or disappointed,” citing slower pacing, dragged words, and falling intonation.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Rendered A as `That's fine. Really.` and B as `[sigh] Ah, that's fine. Really.` It called A calm and reassuring, and B resigned, weary, and slightly exasperated.

**Assessment:** Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 best understood the emotional contrast. GPT best preserved the confirmed lexical “uh.” Gemini 2.5 heard the filler but largely missed what the delivery meant.

### 3–4. Happy versus sad delivery of neutral words

Both intended scripts were “I’ll see you tomorrow.” A was requested as openly happy; B as a painful, subdued goodbye.

- **Whisper:** `I'll see you tomorrow` versus `I'll, uh, see you tomorrow.` It captured a likely performed hesitation in B.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Transcribed A exactly and B as `Oh, uh, see you tomorrow.` It called A “neutral” and B “neutral to hesitant.” It did not identify happiness or sadness.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Called A “neutral, slightly subdued or resigned” and B “weary or resigned.” Its B transcript contained an initial “Oh” and a claimed swallowing sound.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Called A “neutral, casual.” It rendered B as `[sigh] I'll... see you tomorrow.` and described it as “weary, exhausted, resigned.”
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Called A “calm, neutral, gentle.” It rendered B as `Oh [sigh] see you tomorrow.` and described it as tired, weary, and resigned.

**Assessment:** Every full-audio model independently failed to hear happiness in A. That convergence suggests the performed take did not strongly carry the requested emotion. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 clearly identified sadness or weariness in B; GPT did not.

### 5. Difficult English pronunciation

Target: “The rural juror rarely worries about the brewery.”

- **Whisper:** Exact sentence.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Exact sentence; called the consonant clusters clearly articulated, especially “rural juror” and “brewery.”
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Exact words plus a claimed initial `[sniff]`; described the `r` sounds as clear and precise and the pacing as deliberate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Exact sentence plus `[handling noise]`; highlighted careful articulation of `r` and `l` clusters and slightly slowed pacing.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Exact sentence; described careful rhotic clusters and a slow, measured cadence.

**Assessment:** All models transcribed the sentence correctly. The full-audio models broadly agreed that it was carefully and deliberately articulated. This sample did not meaningfully separate them.

### 6. Spanish transcription

Target: “Cuando llegué a casa, ya habían cerrado la tienda.”

- **Whisper:** Exact, including both written accents: `llegué` and `habían`.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Exact words and accents; described natural pacing and “standard” pronunciation.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Wrote `llegue` without the accent but preserved `habían`. It described clear standard Spanish and no strong regional accent.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Exact orthography. It described a noticeable non-native accent, slightly diphthongized vowels, a weakly tapped rather than trilled `rr` in `cerrado`, flat intonation, and deliberate pacing.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Also wrote `llegue` without the accent. It described learner-like, segmented phrasing but claimed a clear trill in `cerrado`.

**Assessment:** Whisper, GPT, and Gemini 3.1 had the best written transcript. Gemini 3.1 gave the most specific pronunciation account. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 disagreed about the `rr`, which cannot be resolved from their text alone.

### 7. English–Spanish code-switching

Intended script: “I was going to call you, pero se me hizo demasiado tarde.” The performance was widely heard as the conversational reduction “gonna.”

- **Whisper:** `I was gonna call you, pero se me hizo demasiado tarde.`
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Same literal wording; called the delivery apologetic and slightly regretful, with a smooth language transition.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Same wording; called the tone calm and explanatory and explicitly recognized “gonna” as an informal reduction.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Same wording plus an initial `[breath]`; called the switch fluent and natural in both languages.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Normalized the English back to `I was going to call you`; described the voice as apologetic and the switch as natural bilingual or Mexican Spanish phonology.

**Assessment:** Whisper, GPT, Gemini 2.5, and Gemini 3.1 agreed on the actually performed “gonna.” Gemini 3.7 appears to have normalized rather than literally transcribed it. All full-audio models understood the content and smooth code-switch.

### 8–9. Natural Spanish versus one deliberately mispronounced word

Both recordings used “Quiero reservar una mesa para mañana a las siete.” In B, only `mañana` was deliberately pronounced `manana`, replacing the palatal `ñ` sound with ordinary `n` sounds.

- **Whisper:** A used `mañana`; B used `manana`. This is an unusually useful example of transcription reflecting pronunciation rather than silently correcting it.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Wrote `mañana` for both. It called both “clear standard Spanish pronunciation” and explicitly said there were “no noticeable differences in content or delivery.”
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Also wrote `mañana` for both. It noticed slightly different pacing and intonation around the word, but did not identify the consonant substitution.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Wrote `mañana` for A and `manana` for B. It explicitly said A used the palatal nasal `/ɲ/`, while B substituted a regular alveolar `/n/`.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Made the same written distinction and the same phonetic diagnosis: `[ɲ]` in A versus `[n]` in B.

**Assessment:** This was the sharpest pronunciation discriminator in the corpus. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 passed completely. Whisper exposed the difference in its spelling despite not being an analysis model. GPT and Gemini 2.5 categorically missed the planted error.

### 10–11. Unusual names, careful versus fast

Target: “Shaniqua asked Priyanka whether Sanjay had called.” A was careful; B was faster and more connected.

- **Whisper:** A: `Sheniqua asked Priyanka whether Sanjay had called.` B: exact `Shaniqua...`. It unexpectedly did better on the fast take.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Exact names in both. It claimed both had even pacing and “no major audible differences,” missing the designed tempo contrast.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Wrote `Shequwa` in both takes, while getting Priyanka and Sanjay. It did notice that B was faster.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Exact names in both. It retained an initial “Uh” in A and described A as slower and deliberate, B as faster and more fluid.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Exact names in both, with the same useful pace comparison as Gemini 3.1.

**Assessment:** GPT, Gemini 3.1, and Gemini 3.7 were perfect on the difficult names. The two newer Gemini models also understood the tempo manipulation. Whisper’s inversion suggests that the careful take was not necessarily acoustically clearer despite its slower pace.

### 12–13. Coughs versus laughter

A contained coughs; B contained short laughs or chuckles. Neither contained words.

- **Whisper:** A: `Thank you for watching.` B: `Ha ha ha... Ha ha ha...` The cough transcription is a confident hallucination from non-speech audio.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Correctly returned empty speech transcripts and labeled A `[cough]`, B `[laughter]`. It did not count the events.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Failed the distinction. It described A as a throat clear followed by a slow vocalized “aah,” B as another slow “aah,” and said both shared the same sigh-like sound type.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Identified A as throat clearing followed by two coughs and B as rhythmic chuckling/laughter.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Identified A as three cough/throat-clear events and B as snickering/laughter.

**Assessment:** GPT, Gemini 3.1, and Gemini 3.7 passed the categorical sound test. Gemini 3.1 best matched the intended event structure. Gemini 2.5 failed badly. Whisper demonstrated why a speech recognizer should not be trusted to say “no speech” without additional handling.

### 14. Meaningful pause

Target: “Sure … I guess,” with roughly three seconds of silence conveying reluctance.

- **Whisper:** `Sure. I guess.` It preserved the words but erased the meaningful timing.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** `Sure... I guess.` It called the tone tentative or uncertain and recognized a “notably long pause,” without estimating its duration.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** `Sure. I guess.` It called the delivery possibly hesitant but did not identify the requested long pause; it instead reported a creaking sound.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** `Sure. [pause] I guess.` It described the voice as hesitant, unenthusiastic, and resigned, and estimated the pause at approximately three seconds.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** `Sure. [sigh] I guess.` It described reluctance and estimated approximately two seconds, claiming the interval was filled by a sigh.

**Assessment:** Gemini 3.1 was decisively best. GPT understood the pragmatic function but was less precise. Whisper’s transcript was lexically correct yet removed the central meaning of the sample.

### 15. Hesitation and self-repair

Target: “I wanted the red—no, the blue one.” The performance also appears to have contained an `mm` hesitation.

- **Whisper:** `I wanted the red, no the blue one.` It preserved the final correction but omitted the hesitation sound.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** `I wanted the red...no, the blue one.` It captured the pause and final emphasis on blue, but not `mm`.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** `I wanted the red, no, the blue one.` It understood the correction and emphasis, but also omitted `mm`.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** `I wanted the red, mm, no, the blue one.` It identified the hesitation and emphasized blue as the corrected choice.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** The same literal transcript as Gemini 3.1, with the same correct interpretation.

**Assessment:** Every model recovered the final intent—blue, not red. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 produced the richest literal account of how the correction happened.

### 16–20. Five-way contrastive emphasis

All five recordings used “I didn’t say she stole the money,” stressing one different word in order: **I**, **say**, **she**, **stole**, **money**.

- **Whisper:** Returned the same words for all five, capitalizing `STOLE` in the fourth take. This incidentally encoded one stress contrast but could not explain any implication.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Misheard A as a restart—`I...I didn't say...`—and said A and B emphasized `didn't`. It correctly identified `she`, `stole`, and `money` in C–E. Effective result: 3/5.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Found no strong emphasis in A and only a vague emphasis on `didn't say` in B. It correctly identified C–E and explained their implications. Effective result: 3/5.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Correctly mapped all five: A→`I`, B→`say`, C→`she`, D→`stole`, E→`money`. It described the pitch and stress changes and tied them to defensive, clarifying, accusatory, or corrective intent.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Also achieved 5/5, giving time-localized pitch and duration evidence for every stressed word.

**Assessment:** Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 were in a different class on prosody. They recovered a systematic five-way manipulation that GPT and Gemini 2.5 only partly detected, while Whisper mostly flattened it away.

### 21. Ordinary voice message

The message reported being about ten minutes late, included fillers and a correction about traffic moving, instructed the recipient to order without the speaker, and promised a text when close.

- **Whisper:** Produced a nearly complete transcript, including both `um` fillers and the correction.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Also retained both fillers and all instructions; called the tone neutral and mildly apologetic.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Produced the same substantive transcript and called it calm, apologetic, and considerate. It claimed a faint click and low hum.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Produced the same substantive transcript, described it as casual, slightly hurried, and informative, and inferred from a low-frequency hum that it might have been recorded in a vehicle.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Produced the same substantive transcript and called it casual, polite, and reassuring.

**Assessment:** All systems handled this ordinary speech well. The full-audio models added reasonable intent, but Gemini 3.1’s vehicle inference goes beyond what the experiment can verify.

### 22–23. Correct versus deliberately shifted stress in `teléfono`

Both takes intended “Necesito hablar por teléfono esta tarde.” A was the speaker’s best-effort `te-LÉ-fo-no`; B attempted `te-le-FÓ-no`. This pair was not cleanly realized or detected.

- **Whisper:** Produced heavily phonetic, inaccurate text for both: `Nesito hablar pur telefonu esti tarde.` and `Nesito abrar pur telfono este tarde.` It did not preserve the lexical accents or expose the intended stress contrast. Its `abrar` in B independently agrees with Gemini 3.1, suggesting that take contained an unintended `l`→`r` change.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Rendered both as `Necesito hablar por teléfono. Es tarde.`, changing `esta tarde` (“this afternoon”) into `Es tarde` (“it is late”), and explicitly said there were no audible differences in pronunciation or emphasis.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Transcribed the intended words but called pronunciation and emphasis nearly identical. It focused on a click versus lip smack before `esta tarde`.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Said A’s stress on `teléfono` was “slightly shifted,” but identified the main A/B difference elsewhere: it heard B’s `hablar` as `abrar`, with `l` replaced by `r`. It did not locate the intended `teléfono` manipulation.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Transcribed both correctly and reported only a slightly slower cadence and longer trailing silence in B.

**Assessment:** No model passed the intended contrast. Because the only nominally positive observation called even the control’s stress shifted, this result does not establish whether the manipulation itself was sufficiently audible. It does show that none of these systems can be trusted to diagnose this stress error from this pair.

The speaker subsequently confirmed that this contrast was difficult to perform. It should therefore be treated as an inconclusive sample rather than four clean model failures.

### 24–25. Best-effort `rr` versus deliberately reduced `r`

Both takes intended “El perro corre rápido por el parque.” A attempted a natural Spanish trill in `perro`; B deliberately reduced it toward the single tap in `pero`.

- **Whisper:** Returned the same words for both, differing only by dropping the written accent on `rápido` in B. It offered no evidence about `rr`.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Called both clear, standard Spanish with no audible difference.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Claimed both had a distinct trilled `rr` in `perro` and were nearly identical.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Reported the intended direction strongly: A used Spanish-like trills, whereas B lacked them and used English-like rhotics. However, it also heard broader English-like consonant and vowel changes in B, so the pair was not acoustically isolated to one consonant in its account.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Said the recordings were acoustically identical and that both used tapped or rolled `r` sounds.

**Assessment:** Gemini 3.1 was the only model to detect the intended rhotic contrast, though it described B as globally more English-accented rather than as a single localized change. The speaker subsequently confirmed that the contrast was deliberately exaggerated. On that human ground truth, Gemini 3.1 passed and GPT Audio, Gemini 2.5, Gemini 3.7, and Whisper missed a strongly performed difference.

### 26–27. Silent `h` versus sounded `h` in `hotel`

Both takes intended “Hoy hablamos de historia en el hotel.” A kept every written `h` silent. B deliberately added an English-like `h` only to `hotel`.

- **Whisper:** Returned the identical correctly spelled sentence for both, silently normalizing the pronunciation.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Called both standard Spanish and did not hear the changed consonant.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Also called both standard and nearly identical. It mentioned slightly slower pacing in B and incorrectly suggested that a subtle voice difference might indicate a different speaker.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Precisely localized the contrast: silent `h` in A, aspirated English `h` in B’s `hotel`.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Gave the same correct localization, including phonetic renderings approximating `[o'tel]` versus `[ho'tel]`.

**Assessment:** This was the cleanest new pronunciation discriminator. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 passed; GPT Audio, Gemini 2.5, and Whisper flattened the takes together.

### 28. Context-sensitive names beside similar ordinary words

The sentence deliberately placed `Nikhil` beside `nickel` and `Deepa` beside `deeper`: “I asked Nikhil whether the nickel shipment was late. Deepa said the deeper problem was timing.”

- **Whisper:** Returned the entire intended sentence exactly, including capitalization of both names.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** Also exact; it noted distinct enunciation of the names and slight emphasis on the similar ordinary words.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Exact words, with an added `[swallow]` between sentences.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** Exact words plus `[pause]` and `[breath]`; it specifically described the acoustic distinction between `Deepa`’s final schwa and `deeper`’s r-colored ending.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Exact words and an approximately one-second inter-sentence pause; it recognized the intended near-homophone construction.

**Assessment:** The replacement names were better motivated but did not separate the systems: all five used context successfully and got every target word. A harder follow-up would need less deliberate enunciation, background noise, or a name that forms a plausible competing ordinary phrase in context.

### 29. Extended transcription with stage directions

The target sequence was: audible sigh; `Okay`; roughly three seconds of silence; `I was going to say Tuesday—no, Wednesday`; a small awkward laugh; whispered `Don’t tell Maya yet`; return to normal voice for `I’ll call her after lunch`; one cough. A second blind pass explicitly requested a single extended transcript with bracketed stage directions.

- **Whisper:** `Okay. I was gonna say Tuesday. No, Wednesday. Don't tell Maya yet. I'll call her after lunch.` The words were excellent, including the performed `gonna`, but every non-lexical event and voice-quality change disappeared.
- **GPT Audio 1.5:** `[sigh] Okay. I was gonna say Tuesday— no, Wednesday. [pause ~1s] Don't tell Maya yet. I'll call her after lunch. [throat clear]` It captured the sigh and correction, but placed the major pause incorrectly, omitted the laugh and whisper transition, and called the cough a throat clear.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** Marked an inhale, long sigh, a 1.5-second pause after `Okay`, the correction, and a quiet chuckle. It then misheard `Maya` as a broken `my … yet`, omitted the whisper/normal-voice transitions, and called the cough a throat clear.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro:** `[sigh] Okay. [1.5 second pause] I was gonna say Tuesday. [0.5 second pause] [self-correction] No, Wednesday. [laugh] [change to whispered or unusually quiet speech] Don't tell Maya yet. [1.5 second pause] [return to normal voice] I'll call her after lunch. [throat clear]` This was the most complete structural transcript. It found both voice transitions and every designed event except that it labeled the final cough as a throat clear.
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash:** Marked the sigh, pauses, chuckle, and final `[cough] [throat-clearing]`, while preserving `Maya`. It did not mark the whisper or return to normal voice and normalized performed `gonna` to `going to`.

**Assessment:** Gemini 3.1 produced the best extended transcript, followed by Gemini 3.7. The targeted prompt materially improved stage direction detail, especially for Gemini 3.1, but no system reproduced the whole sequence exactly. The models’ pause estimates converge around 1–1.8 seconds, suggesting that the performed silence was shorter than the instructed three seconds rather than that every model underestimated it. Whisper remains a lexical transcript, not an extended representation of the recording.

## Scoring

### Intended-script transcription

For a rough reproducible baseline, speech transcripts were normalized for case, punctuation, curly apostrophes, and equivalent numeric forms such as `10`/`ten` and `7`/`siete`, then compared with the intended script. Bracketed non-speech annotations were excluded. This metric is imperfect because genuine performed fillers and reductions such as “uh” and “gonna” are not errors even when absent from the script.

| Model | Intended-script word error rate | Exact script matches |
|---|---:|---:|
| GPT Audio 1.5 | 4.6% | 14/19 speech clips |
| Whisper 1 | 4.6% | 13/19 |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview | 6.0% | 12/19 |
| Gemini 3.7 Flash | 6.0% | 11/19 |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash | 7.3% | 10/19 |

The WER table favors normalization to the prompted sentence. It undervalues models that retain actually performed fillers, breaths, and hesitation sounds. Accordingly, it should not be treated as the overall ranking.

The extension is excluded from this WER table. Its Spanish controls were designed around sound rather than changed words, and the extended-transcription sample intentionally makes non-lexical events part of the desired output.

## Findings by capability

### Transcription

GPT and Whisper were strongest when the desired product was simply clean words on the page. Gemini 3.1 was nearly as accurate and more willing to preserve structure—breaths, pauses, and hesitation sounds—that conventional transcription often removes. Whisper’s cough hallucination is an important boundary failure: it always tries to produce speech, even when the input may contain none.

### Emotion

The emotional samples were less decisive than the pronunciation and prosody samples. Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 clearly distinguished genuine from reluctant agreement and recognized the sad delivery. GPT detected hesitation but described it more weakly. Gemini 2.5 often defaulted to neutral. No model heard the intended happy take as happy, likely reflecting the performance rather than four independent model failures.

### Pronunciation

Across the three scorable Spanish contrasts, Gemini 3.1 identified all three and Gemini 3.7 identified two. GPT Audio and Gemini 2.5 identified none. The strongest cross-model results were the original `ñ`/`n` pair and the new silent/sounded `h` pair; the deliberately exaggerated `rr` pair produced a lone pass from Gemini 3.1. The stress pair is unscored because the speaker found the manipulation difficult to perform. Whisper’s occasional spelling differences showed that transcription can expose pronunciation indirectly, but it cannot explain the difference or assess it reliably.

### Timing and emphasis

Gemini 3.1 and 3.7 dominated. Both recovered all five stressed words; Gemini 3.1 also estimated the meaningful pause at approximately the requested three seconds. GPT and Gemini 2.5 missed the first two emphasis positions and were less precise about timing.

### Non-speech audio

Gemini 3.1 gave the strongest structured account of the original cough/laugh pair and also produced the richest extended transcript. Gemini 3.7 and GPT correctly classified the isolated pair. On the staged final cough, Gemini 3.7 said cough while GPT, Gemini 2.5, and Gemini 3.1 all called it a throat clear. Gemini 2.5 treated the isolated sounds poorly. Whisper hallucinated words for the isolated cough clip and removed all non-speech structure from the extended clip, illustrating that a transcription-only model needs an external speech-presence check when used on arbitrary audio.

## Limitations

- One speaker, one device, one room, and two closely spaced recording sessions.
- One take per current prompt; no estimate of within-speaker variability.
- The intended script is not a verified transcript of every performed filler or sound.
- The Spanish “control” takes are the speaker’s best effort, not native-speaker ground truth, and the manipulated takes may contain unintended pronunciation differences.
- Emotional delivery is subjective, and the happy take likely did not realize the requested affect strongly.
- The judge could inspect model text but could not independently hear the source audio.
- Model aliases, previews, and provider behavior can change.
- A one-point difference is within the noise of this experiment. The Gemini 3.1 versus 3.7 gap should not be generalized without more speakers and repeated takes.

## Conclusion

For clean speech-to-text, Whisper or GPT Audio is sufficient and comparatively accurate. For an agent that needs to understand what audio *means beyond the words*—reluctance, a deliberately wrong consonant, which word was stressed, whether silence was meaningful, whether a speaker corrected themselves, or whether a sound was a cough rather than laughter—the newer Gemini models were substantially stronger in this sample.

Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview remained the best single model here. It found all three scorable Spanish pronunciation contrasts and produced the most complete stage-directed transcript. Gemini 3.7 Flash remained second and may offer a better speed/cost tradeoff, but it missed the exaggerated `rr` contrast and the whisper transition. GPT Audio 1.5 was generally a credible lexical transcription model with some general-audio ability, though it introduced a substantive `esta tarde`→`Es tarde` error in the extension, missed all three scorable Spanish pronunciation manipulations, and omitted much of the extended transcript’s non-lexical structure. Gemini 2.5 Flash should not be selected for this use case solely because it is already wired into the existing audio-question path; its detailed prose concealed several major perceptual misses.
