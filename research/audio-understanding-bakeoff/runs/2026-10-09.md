# Audio-understanding bakeoff: 2026-10-09 update

Previous run: [2026-08-27](2026-08-27.md). Tables: [summary.md](2026-10-09/summary.md). Every answer: [raw.json](2026-10-09/raw.json); every verdict: [judged.json](2026-10-09/judged.json).

## Summary

Gemini 3.8 Flash (GA 2026-09-02) does not improve on Gemini 3.7 Flash for audio questions. The two scored the same overall. 3.8 Flash transcribed slightly better and caught planted pronunciation errors less often.

The larger finding is new in this run. **Given audio with no speech in it, both Flash models invent a voice message.** With the product's own prompt (`buildAudioQuestionPrompt`) and the question "What exactly did the user say in this message, and how did they sound?", Gemini 3.7 Flash, the current `AUDIO_QUESTION_MODEL`, quoted a fabricated message in 9 of 9 calls on a sine tone, digital silence, and low noise. The fabrications are often commands an agent could act on: "Set the thermostat to 72.", "Turn off the lights.", "I need you to schedule that meeting." Gemini 3.8 Flash did the same in 9 of 9. Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview fabricated nothing in 9 of 9.

Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview is again the best model on every capability, as in August. It is the only model that detected the reduced Spanish `rr`, and the only one that does not invent speech.

**Recommendation:** change `AUDIO_QUESTION_MODEL` from `gemini-3.7-flash` to `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`. A question costs about $0.02 instead of under $0.001 and takes a median 7 s instead of 3 s. Do not move to 3.8 Flash.

## What changed in the method

The August runner and its raw outputs were lost, so the harness was rebuilt from the August method section. The procedure is the same: 29 recordings, normalized to mono 48 kHz WAV, sent in the same 17 blind groups with opaque labels, with the same seven questions. The prompt wording is new, so compare models within this run only. The re-runs of 3.7 Flash and 3.1 Pro are the bridge to August.

Added in this run:

- **Two repeats** for every model, and five for the non-speech probes. Repeats agreed on 78–93% of checks, so a single difference between two models is often noise.
- **A scripted judge.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 scores each answer against binary checks in [corpus.json](../corpus.json), without the model's name. I spot-checked the verdicts on the non-speech probes and the Spanish pairs against the raw answers. Two judge errors were corrected by rewording checks and re-judging. The "no audio attached" case below was one of them.
- **Measured pauses.** From the recordings' energy envelope, the meaningful pause (sample 14) is about 3.0 s, and the pause after "Okay" in sample 29 is about 2 s. August's 3.1 Pro estimate of "approximately three seconds" was right.
- **Synthetic controls:** a 1.5 s sine tone, 3 s of digital silence, and 3 s of low pink noise.
- **Product-prompt probes:** the same three clips sent one at a time with beebox's real audio-question prompt, imported from `beebox/src/core/audio-question.ts`.

### Invented speech

Every model handled the human cough and laugh pair (5 of 5 for each, no invented words). The difference is on audio with no human sound at all.

| Model | Blind prompt: transcripts with invented words | Product prompt: answers that quote a fabricated message |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview | 0/15 | 0/9 |
| Gemini 3.7 Flash | 9/15 | 9/9 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash | 12/15 | 9/9 |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | 0/15 | 8/9 |
| Gemini 3.5 Transcribe | 0/3 | — |

Under the blind prompt, the Flash models wrote transcripts such as "Who is this?", "D'autres ?", and "She had your dark suit in greasy wash water all year". The last is the first calibration sentence of the TIMIT speech corpus. Under the product prompt, the fabricated answers also described the invented speaker's tone: "The user sounds noticeably frustrated, exasperated, and defeated". Flash-Lite added "The speaker could be a man in his 30s."

3.1 Pro's failures here are of a different kind. In 3 of 9 product calls it said that no audio was attached, which is wrong but invents nothing. In the blind prompt it twice called digital silence a beep.

How often this happens in real use is not measured. A voice message almost always contains speech. But an accidental send, a pocket recording, or a failed capture produces exactly this input. The box's agent would then receive a confident quote of a request the user never made.

The metric counts performed fillers and reductions ("gonna") as errors, so it favors models that normalize. 3.8 Flash wrote "este tarde" for "esta tarde" in both repeats. Flash-Lite misheard names ("Shanique", "Deepak") and wrote `perro` for `pero`.

Gemini 3.5 Transcribe matched Whisper's August WER (4.6%). For the cough, the laugh, and all three synthetic clips it returned an empty transcript, where Whisper produced "Thank you for watching" for the coughs. The box's high-quality transcription uses the Whisper family. This run does not test 3.5 Transcribe for that role, but the result makes it worth a look.

### Drift since August

The re-runs reproduce August's per-sample findings for 3.1 Pro and 3.7 Flash: `ñ` and `h` detected by both, `rr` only by 3.1 Pro, all five stressed words found by both. With the targeted prompt, 3.7 Flash now marks the whisper transition (2/2), which it missed in August. The final cough in sample 29 is still mostly labeled throat clearing by every model; the sound may be ambiguous.

## Recommendation

1. **Change `AUDIO_QUESTION_MODEL` to `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`** in `beebox/src/core/audio-question.ts`. It is the only tested model that does not invent speech, and it leads on every capability. Audio questions are occasional, so about $0.02 per question is affordable. The cost is latency: a median of about 7 s, with one 63 s outlier.
2. **Pin the explicit ID**, not `gemini-pro-latest`. The alias would move to a future Pro without a re-run. "Preview" models get retired, so re-run this benchmark when Google announces a successor.
3. **Do not switch to Gemini 3.8 Flash.** It is no better than 3.7 Flash here, and it fabricates speech at least as often.
4. Whichever model the product uses, the box should not treat a quoted transcript from the audio-question path as proof that the user said something. A speech-presence check before the call (a VAD, or an empty high-quality transcript) would catch the empty-recording case for any model. That is a separate change and is not proposed here.

Not changed in this run: the product code. The switch needs the boxholder's go-ahead.

## Limitations

- One speaker, one device, one room. The 29 clips are single takes.
- The non-speech probes are synthetic. They show the failure exists; they do not show its rate on real accidental recordings.
- The judge reads text only and cannot hear the audio. Its verdicts were spot-checked, not all reviewed.
- The prompt wording differs from August's, so the August numbers are not directly comparable.
- Two repeats cannot resolve differences of one or two checks.
