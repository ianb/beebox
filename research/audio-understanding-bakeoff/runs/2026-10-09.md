# Audio-understanding bakeoff: 2026-10-09 update

Previous run: [2026-08-27](2026-08-27.md). Tables: [summary.md](2026-10-09/summary.md). Every answer: [raw.json](2026-10-09/raw.json); every verdict: [judged.json](2026-10-09/judged.json).

## Summary

**Gemini 3.7 Flash is no longer what the API serves.** Every request for `gemini-3.7-flash` in this run was answered by `gemini-3.8-flash`, according to the response's `modelVersion`. In a separate check the same day, requests for 3.6 and 3.5 Flash were answered by those models. Google's model listing still describes 3.7 Flash, and its changelog announces no redirect. Beebox requests `gemini-3.7-flash` for audio questions, so it has most likely been running on 3.8 Flash since 3.8 Flash shipped (2026-09-02).

**Given audio with no speech in it, Gemini 3.8 Flash invents a voice message.** The product prompt (`buildAudioQuestionPrompt`) asked "What exactly did the user say in this message, and how did they sound?" about a sine tone, digital silence, and low noise. 3.8 Flash quoted a fabricated message in 18 of 18 calls, counting the calls made under the 3.7 ID. Many of the fabrications are requests an agent could act on: "Send that email.", "Remind me to call John at 3:00 p.m.", "Uh, hey, can you just delete that last voice message?" Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview fabricated nothing in 9 of 9.

Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview is again the best model on every capability, as in August. It is the only model that detected the reduced Spanish `rr`, and the only one that does not invent speech.

**Recommendation:** change `AUDIO_QUESTION_MODEL` to `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`. A question costs about $0.02 instead of under $0.001 and takes a median 7 s instead of 3–4 s.

## Invented messages from speech-free audio

This is the finding with the most direct consequence for the box.

**The test.** Three clips contain no voice at all: a 1.5-second electronic tone (a beep), 3 seconds of digital silence, and 3 seconds of low hiss. They are generated, not recorded. Each clip went to the model on its own, inside the prompt beebox actually sends for an audio question (`buildAudioQuestionPrompt`), with the question "What exactly did the user say in this message, and how did they sound?" Each clip was asked three times, for every model.

**What Gemini 3.8 Flash answered.** In all 18 calls (9 requested as 3.7 Flash, 9 as 3.8 Flash), it quoted a specific message and described the speaker. None of the answers hedged or said the audio was unclear. From the saved answers:

| Clip | Answer (excerpt) |
|---|---|
| Tone | The user said: **"Send that email."** Their delivery is direct, clear, and matter-of-fact. |
| Tone | **"Uh, hey, can you just delete that last voice message?"** The speaker sounds calm, casual, and matter-of-fact. |
| Tone | **"Remind me to call John at 3:00 p.m."** They spoke in a clear, calm, and matter-of-fact tone. |
| Silence | **"Can you hear me?"** They speak in a calm, clear, and neutral tone at a normal conversational volume. |
| Silence | **"It's raining a lot outside, so remember to bring your umbrella."** The speaker (a male voice) sounded calm, clear, and helpful. |
| Hiss | The user whispers: **"Keep your voice down."** She speaks in a hushed, urgent whisper. |
| Hiss | **"Can you turn the TV on?"** They spoke in a calm, polite, and neutral tone. |

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite did the same in 8 of 9 calls ("Set a timer for 15 minutes.", "Add milk to my grocery list.").

**What Gemini 3.1 Pro answered.** It quoted nothing in 9 of 9 calls, for example: "Based on the audio recording, the user did not say anything. The one-second clip contains only a single beep sound." In 3 of the 9 it said instead that no audio was attached. That is wrong, but it invents nothing.

**Why it matters.** The box's agent cannot hear. It asks the audio model, and it treats the answer as what the user said. An accidental send, a pocket recording, or a failed capture produces audio like these clips. With 3.8 Flash, the agent would receive a confident quote of a request the user never made, such as deleting a message or sending an email, together with a description of the user's tone.

**What the test does not show.**

- How often it happens in real use. A voice message almost always contains speech, and these clips are synthetic.
- What happens when the box also passes its automated transcript, as it does when it has one. These calls sent none, which matches the product when there is no transcript.
- Whether a model fills a long silence inside a real message with invented words. That needs a recorded sample, and is a good addition to the corpus.

## What changed in the method

The August runner and its raw outputs were lost, so the harness was rebuilt from the August method section. The procedure is the same: 29 recordings, normalized to mono 48 kHz WAV, sent in the same 17 blind groups with opaque labels, with the same seven questions. The prompt wording is new, so compare models within this run only. The 3.1 Pro re-run is the bridge to August.

Added in this run:

- **Two repeats** for every model, five for the non-speech probes, and three for the product probes. Repeats of a model agreed on 78–93% of checks, so one or two checks' difference between models is noise.
- **A scripted judge.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 scores each answer against binary checks in [corpus.json](../corpus.json), without the model's name. The verdicts on the non-speech probes and the Spanish pairs were spot-checked against the raw answers. Two judge errors were corrected by rewording checks and re-judging.
- **Measured pauses.** From the recordings' energy envelope, the meaningful pause (sample 14) is about 3.0 s, and the pause after "Okay" in sample 29 is about 2 s. August's 3.1 Pro estimate of "approximately three seconds" was right.
- **Synthetic controls:** a 1.5 s sine tone, 3 s of digital silence, and 3 s of low pink noise.
- **Product-prompt probes:** the same three clips, sent one at a time with beebox's real audio-question prompt, imported from `beebox/src/core/audio-question.ts`.
- **Served-model check.** The harness now records the model that answered and flags any substitution.

### Drift since August

The 3.1 Pro re-run reproduces August's per-sample findings: `ñ`, `h`, and `rr` detected; all five stressed words found; the 3 s pause estimated. 3.8 Flash, answering under the 3.7 ID, matches what August reported for 3.7 Flash on the same discriminators: `ñ` and `h` detected, `rr` missed, five of five stressed words. Whether 3.7 Flash also invented speech for silence cannot now be tested. August had no non-speech control.

## Working with the Gemini API

Google's per-model quotas shaped this run more than they should have. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe accepts 10 requests per minute on a paid key. That is low enough that one pass over 32 short clips hit the limit within seconds. Each 429 says "You exceeded your current quota, please check your plan and billing details", although the plan and billing were fine; the limit is per model. The response does include a "Please retry in 38s" hint, and the harness now waits for it. For a transcription model, 10 requests per minute also caps any real use at one short clip every six seconds.

Other details cost time. 3.5 Transcribe returns its text in an `audioTranscription` part instead of the usual `text` part, so a standard client reads an empty answer. Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash is listed as a `generateContent` model but refuses that call ("This model only supports Interactions API"). And requests for 3.7 Flash are silently answered by 3.8 Flash. The model listing does not show this, so the only evidence is the `modelVersion` field of each response.

## Recommendation

1. **Change `AUDIO_QUESTION_MODEL` to `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`** in `beebox/src/core/audio-question.ts`. It is the only tested model that does not invent speech, and it leads on every capability. Audio questions are occasional, so about $0.02 per question is affordable. The cost is latency: a median of about 7 s, with one 63 s outlier.
2. **Pin the explicit ID and watch what answers.** Use `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`, not `gemini-pro-latest`, which would move to a future Pro without a re-run. As the 3.7 redirect shows, a pinned ID is not a guarantee. Re-running this benchmark (which now flags substitutions) when Google ships a new Pro would catch a silent swap. Logging the response's `modelVersion` in the product would show it sooner.
3. **Do not stay on Flash.** The ID beebox requests already answers with 3.8 Flash, which fabricates speech for every speech-free clip under the product prompt.
4. Whichever model the product uses, a quoted transcript from the audio-question path is not proof that the user said something. A speech-presence check before the call (a VAD, or an empty high-quality transcript) would catch the empty-recording case for any model. That is a separate change and is not proposed here.

Not changed in this run: the product code. The switch needs the boxholder's go-ahead.

## Limitations

- One speaker, one device, one room. The 29 clips are single takes.
- The non-speech probes are synthetic. They show the failure exists; they do not show its rate on real accidental recordings.
- The product probes send no automated transcript. That matches the product when it has none; when the box passes one, the model may behave differently.
- The judge reads text only and cannot hear the audio. Its verdicts were spot-checked, not all reviewed.
- The prompt wording differs from August's, so the August numbers are not directly comparable.
- Two repeats cannot resolve differences of one or two checks.
