# Authentic AI-Assisted Writing: Process and Provenance

Research synthesis, 2026-07-19. Focus: how people keep authorship with the human when AI helps produce a document — process (how the human's material gets in and stays in) and provenance (knowing which words are whose). Style cloning / voice mimicry deliberately excluded.

Each claim is labeled: **[empirical]** (peer-reviewed or measured), **[practice]** (established professional norm/standard), **[opinion]** (practitioner essay, manifesto, blog), **[proposal]** (prototype/spec not adopted). Preprints and secondhand sources are flagged inline.

---

## 1. Vocabulary: is there a term for "AI assembles the human's words"?

**Short answer: no established single term exists.** This is an underserved conceptual niche. The closest framings:

- **Centaur vs. cyborg (vs. self-automator).** The most developed taxonomy of human-AI division of labor, from general knowledge-work research, not writing specifically. Centaur = clean macro-level task boundaries (human keeps whole tasks); cyborg = continuous micro-blending at the sentence level; self-automator = abdication. An HBS working paper (Randazzo, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Dell'Acqua, Mollick, Candelon, Lakhani; 244 BCG consultants) formalizes these as "Fused / Directed / Abdicated" co-creation; centaurs achieved the highest accuracy by retaining judgment over confident-but-wrong AI output. **[empirical]** [HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=68273), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4921696). Mollick applies it to his own writing: centaur work has "a clear line between person and machine"; he used AI extensively around *Co-Intelligence* but not for the prose itself. **[opinion]** [oneusefulthing.org](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/i-cyborg-using-co-intelligence)
- **"Writing with AI" vs. "writing by AI"** is live but contested. James O'Sullivan argues the "with" framing is a rhetorical cleanse: "the degree of a writer's involvement in selecting and revising the model's output" doesn't change the output's origins — if the distinction really mattered, people wouldn't conceal AI use. **[opinion]** [jamescosullivan.substack.com](https://jamescosullivan.substack.com/p/writing-with-ai-is-the-same-as-writing-by-ai). Note: this critique targets *selecting/revising the model's output* — it does not reach a practice where the words originate with the human, which is a point in the boxholder's design's favor and worth stating explicitly when describing the practice.
- **Augmentation vs. automation** — the general business-AI frame (automation removes the human; augmentation keeps them in the loop). Maps loosely onto assembly vs. generation but the sources are generic workforce framing. **[opinion]** [number6.ai](https://number6.ai/blog/11-ai-automation-vs-ai-augmentation/)
- **"Thought partner"** — Mina Lee et al. propose it as an alternative to "tool," emphasizing adaptation to the human's reasoning over task completion. **[opinion/perspective]** [UChicago DSI](https://datascience.uchicago.edu/news/rethinking-ai-as-a-thought-partner-perspectives-on-writing-programming-and-more/)
- **"Provocateur," not assistant** — Advait Sarkar's "AI Should Challenge, Not Obey" (CACM 2024): AI that "does not complete your report, does not draft your email... but rather critiques your work." Adjacent (critique vs. assembly) but the closest published manifesto to the boxholder's stance. **[opinion]** [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/opinion/ai-should-challenge-not-obey/), [arXiv:2411.02263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02263). His "Intention Is All You Need" (PPIG 2024) argues generative AI exerts a "mechanised convergence" on user intention itself — an assembly-only AI sidesteps this because it never substitutes its intention for the user's words. **[empirical/theoretical]** [arXiv:2410.18851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18851)
- The **trade vocabulary of ghostwriting** is a credit-line hierarchy, not a process term: "A and W" > "A with W" > "A, as told to W" > uncredited ghost. "As told to" is the one existing label that explicitly frames the writer's role as secondary/transcriptive. **[practice]** [Lee Barnathan](https://leebarnathan.com/blog/writing/ghostwriter-co-writer-co-author-or-collaborator/), [Writers and Editors](https://www.writersandeditors.com/book_collaboration__ghostwriting_57408.htm)
- The **editing profession's** vocabulary (Section 4) — "copyediting" vs. "substantive editing" vs. "rewriting" — is the most precise existing language for the boundary the boxholder cares about.
- HCI systematic reviews confirm the field taxonomizes by *stage and intervention level*, not by a generate/assemble binary — "Co-Writing with AI, on Human Terms" (109-paper review + 15 interviews) finds desired intervention varies by writing stage and writer type. **[empirical]** [arXiv:2504.12488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12488)

## 2. The empirical core: what AI drafts do to opinion, voice, and ownership

This is the best-evidenced part of the whole question, and it substantially supports the boxholder's design instincts.

### 2.1 AI suggestions shift what the human says — without the human noticing

- **Jakesch, Bhat, Buschek, Zalmanson, Naaman — "Co-Writing with Opinionated Language Models Affects Users' Views" (CHI 2023, N=1,506).** Participants writing an essay with a covertly biased assistant were **twice as likely** to adopt the AI's position in a post-task attitude survey vs. unassisted controls; independent judges (N=500) confirmed the essays expressed the assistant's slant. Participants attributed the opinions to themselves — the persuasion was latent. The single strongest number in the space. **[empirical]** [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581196), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00560), [Cornell coverage](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2023/05/writing-ai-help-can-shift-your-opinions)
- **"Reactive Writers" (CHI 2026; 19 interviews + traces of 1,291 co-writing sessions)** names the mechanism: writers shift from generative ideation ("what do I think?") to evaluation-first "reactive writing" ("do I agree with this suggestion?"). ~25–31% of final essay text came directly from AI suggestions; AI-side topical bias measurably carried into participants' own final text; time savings were modest (~7.5–10%); and writers **felt in control throughout** — a "paradox of control." **[empirical]** [arXiv:2603.10374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10374), [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3772318.3791529)

### 2.2 Homogenization: individually better, collectively narrower

- **Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances 2024).** GPT-4 story ideas raised individual story quality (+3.7% with one idea, +9.0% with five, gains concentrated among less-creative writers) but AI-assisted stories were measurably more similar to one another — a social-dilemma structure. **[empirical]** [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)
- **Padmakumar & He (ICLR 2024).** Co-writing with a **feedback-tuned (RLHF)** model significantly reduced content diversity across authors; co-writing with base GPT-3 did not — implicating instruction-tuning specifically as the homogenizing force. **[empirical]** [arXiv:2309.05196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196)
- **Agarwal, Naaman, et al. (CHI 2025, N=118 India+US).** With AI suggestions, Indian participants' writing became measurably more American — a classifier distinguishing Indian vs. American authorship performed worse on AI-assisted essays. Framed as AI-driven cultural homogenization. **[empirical]** [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713564), [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/04/ai-suggestions-make-writing-more-generic-western)
- Open mechanism question / disagreement: nobody has isolated whether prompting style, tuning, or sheer fluency drives homogenization; Padmakumar & He's base-model null suggests it isn't generation per se.

### 2.3 Ownership: fragile, UI-contingent, and dissociated from disclosure

- **Draxler et al. — "The AI Ghostwriter Effect" (ACM TOCHI 2024; n=30, n=96).** People using AI-generated personalized text did **not** feel ownership of it internally, yet self-declared as authors and were reluctant to disclose AI involvement. What moved felt ownership was **the degree of the participant's own edited influence** — not personalization of the AI output. **[empirical]** [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3637875), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03283)
- **Lehmann, Markert, Dang, Buschek (MuC 2022, N=18).** Interface design changes perceived authorship independent of content: suggestion-list UI → people wrote less but still felt like the author; continuous word-by-word generation → perceived authorship dropped. Ownership is an interaction-design variable, not a fixed property of "using AI." **[empirical]** [arXiv:2208.00870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00870)
- **"Who Owns the Text?" (2026 preprint, N=176).** Psychological ownership dropped ~0.85–1.0 points (7-pt scale) in AI-assisted conditions even with similar quality and lower cognitive load; persona coaching did not rescue it; style personalization only partially (+0.43). Proposes five design patterns: on-demand initiation, micro-suggestions, voice anchoring, audience scaffolds, **point-of-decision provenance**. **[empirical, preprint]** [arXiv:2601.10236](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10236)
- **"Where Do I 'Add the Egg'?" (CHI 2026, N=18).** System metaphor (agentic/tool-like/magical) shifts where writers locate their ownership; the titular "add the egg" pattern — minimal token effort restoring felt authorship, from pancake-mix marketing — is a warning: felt authorship can be manufactured cheaply and is not the same as real authorship. **[empirical]** [PDF](https://jebthomas.ca/assets/pdf/egg.pdf), [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791952)
- **Task-delegation drift:** "I am not thinking anymore, just following the path" — over half of studied authors delegated to the point of only minor adjustments, with felt loss of agency; distinguishes delegation from agency-preserving co-creation. **[empirical]** [arXiv:2502.16740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16740)
- **MIT Media Lab "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (Kosmyna et al., preprint, N=54, EEG).** LLM-assisted essay writers showed the weakest neural connectivity, the **lowest self-reported ownership**, and "struggled to accurately quote their own work" minutes after writing it; brain-only writers highest on all three. Widely covered; **not peer-reviewed**, small N, and the "cognitive debt" framing has drawn methodological criticism — but the ownership/self-quoting two-arm difference is fairly clean. **[empirical, preprint — flagged]** [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/), [limitations summary](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/learn-about-the-this-is-your-brain-on-chatgpt-study-results-limitations-risks-and-more)

### 2.4 Anchoring: does seeing a draft fix you to it? (the boxholder's hypothesis)

Direct evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive** — the clean experiment ("AI asks questions" vs. "AI hands you a draft," measured on ownership/voice) has apparently not been run. What exists:

- **Design-fixation, adjacent domain:** Wadinambiarachchi et al. (CHI 2024, N=60) — exposure to an AI-generated example during ideation increased fixation on it; the AI-supported group produced **fewer, less varied, less original** ideas than no-AI baseline. Image ideation, not text drafts; extrapolation is reasonable but untested. **[empirical]** [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642919)
- **Reactive Writers** (above) is effectively anchoring evidence for text: suggestion exposure flips the writer into evaluate-mode and ~25–31% of the AI's words survive into the final text unnoticed. **[empirical]**
- **Gero — "Sparks" (In2Writing 2022).** Deliberately anti-anchoring design: short, decontextualized, incomplete fragments meant to provoke rather than be copied. No head-to-head quantitative comparison vs. draft-style tools. **[empirical, qualitative]** [PDF](https://www.katygero.com/papers/2022_Sparks.pdf), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.in2writing-1.12/)
- **Professional writers resist drafts even when offered:** Wordcraft (Google, 13 pro writers, 8 weeks) — valued as brainstorming partner, not drafter; complained of formulaic style. Dramatron (CHI 2023, 15 theatre/film pros) — impressive but "formulaic," lacking subtext; would not use it to draft a complete work. **[empirical]** [Wordcraft paper](https://wordcraft-writers-workshop.appspot.com/paper/wordcraft_writers_workshop_paper.pdf), [Dramatron](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3544548.3581225)
- **Question-only AI can work but isn't compared:** "When AI only asks" (Frontiers in Education 2026, N=17 students) — a question-only prewriting chatbot; concrete/sensory/memory questions anchored elaboration better than abstract reflective ones; some students disengaged. **No draft-condition control arm.** **[empirical, small-N]** [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1740044/full). VISAR deployment (N=49): students given planning scaffolds rather than drafts engaged with them rather than bypassing them; observational only. **[empirical, preprint]** [arXiv:2603.15777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15777)
- Socratic-tutoring literature (questions beat answers for learning) is abundant but has not been transplanted into a writing-ownership frame. **[thin]**
- A practitioner data point from ghostwriting: Josh Bernoff argues AI drafts are actively harmful to ghostwriting because drafting **bypasses the interview** — and the interview is where the telling material the subject didn't know was worth mentioning comes from. Recovering the client's voice from under an AI draft is more work than eliciting it fresh. **[opinion, single practitioner]** [bernoff.com](https://bernoff.com/blog/should-you-use-ai-to-create-a-draft-for-your-ghostwriter)

**Verdict on the hypothesis:** anchoring-to-drafts is supported by converging indirect evidence (design fixation, reactive-writing mode-flip, latent opinion shift, pro writers' revealed preference against drafts) and contradicted by nothing found. But "questions beat drafts" as a measured comparison is an open empirical gap — honest framing is "well-motivated by adjacent evidence, not yet directly demonstrated."

### 2.5 Tension worth holding

The lab findings (everyday writers absorb AI framing without noticing) vs. the pro-writer studies (Wordcraft/Dramatron participants actively resist drafts) are plausibly reconciled by stakes and voice-investment: people with a developed voice and skin in the game resist anchoring; casual/low-stakes writers do not. A tool for non-professional writers should therefore assume **no** natural resistance to anchoring — the protection has to come from the process design. Writers' values research supports this: even AI-heavy creative writers rank authentic self-expression and love-of-process as core values (Guo et al., In2Writing 2024 **[empirical, N=8]** [PDF](https://aliciaguo.com/papers/writer_values.pdf); Gero et al., CHI 2023 on social dynamics of support **[empirical]** [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3580782)).

## 3. Process norms from professions that already do this

Three fields have spent decades turning one person's speech into that person's prose. Their norms converge on a small set of principles.

### 3.1 Ghostwriting / "as-told-to"

- **The interview is the creative act, not a preliminary.** Memoir ghostwriting practice treats the recorded interview series as where the book comes from; the writing is shaping that material. **[practice]** [thewritersforhire.com](https://www.thewritersforhire.com/how-a-memoir-ghostwriter-can-help-you-shape-your-memoir/). The best-documented high-end case: J.R. Moehringer lived a mile from Agassi for two years, including watching old matches while Agassi narrated his in-match thoughts shot-by-shot. **[practice]** [thewritingking.com](https://thewritingking.com/moehringer-agassi/). Moehringer's role-definition (via William Gass): the ghost is "the air in someone else's trumpet." **[opinion]** [Gotham Ghostwriters](https://gothamghostwriters.com/j-r-moehringer-reveals-future-of-ghostwriting/)
- **The trade's bright line: shape, don't invent.** Converging practitioner/ethics sources: a ghost may select, sequence, and dramatize material the subject actually said or experienced, and verify small facts — but must not invent experiences, expertise, or opinions the subject never had. **[practice]** [professionalghost.com](https://professionalghost.com/is-ghostwriting-ethical/), [Saturday Evening Post](https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2025/09/is-ghostwriting-ethical/)
- **The canonical failure mode is curation, not fabrication.** Tony Schwartz / *The Art of the Deal*: Schwartz invented no facts but curated and amplified a persona he later called false — "I put lipstick on a pig. I created a character far more winning than Trump actually is"; "the greatest regret of my life." **[documented practitioner testimony]** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tony-schwartz-trumps-ghostwriter-says-writing-the-art-of-the-deal-is-the-greatest-regret-of-his-life/). Lesson for the design: *selection and arrangement are themselves authorial acts capable of falsifying a person* — provenance of words is necessary but not sufficient; the human must also own the selection.
- Trade commentary acknowledges the occupational tension openly — ghosts "know much of their writing is untrue" in the shaped/dramatized sense (Peter Bart, Deadline, headline-level access only). **[opinion, secondhand]** [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2023/05/prince-harry-spare-ghostwriter-j-r-moehringer-peter-bart-1235379003/)
- Ghostwriters on AI: essentially one substantive essay found (Bernoff, §2.4 above) — AI drafts read generic, smuggle unattributed material, and bypass the elicitation where authenticity comes from. **[opinion, thin area]**

### 3.2 Oral history — the field that has worked this exact problem longest

- **The verbatim/edited spectrum is codified**: full verbatim (every um and false start — used when verbal behavior is itself data) → intelligent verbatim (filler stripped, voice and meaningful pauses kept) → edited/clean read (smoothed for publication). Widely stated rule of thumb: **false starts that carry meaning are preserved (broken with an em-dash) even when pure filler is dropped**, because false starts reflect thought patterns and filler doesn't. Institutions genuinely disagree on where exactly to draw the line — a live debate, not settled. **[practice, taxonomy partly via a commercial vendor's practice guides]** [GoTranscript](https://gotranscript.com/en/blog/clean-verbatim-vs-verbatim-oral-history)
- **Brackets `[ ]` are reserved exclusively for editorial insertions** — words not on the recording ([laughter], [inaudible], expanded acronyms). Near-universal convention (Baylor Institute for Oral History style guide, the field's most-cited; also Margaret Walker Center, Guilford, etc.). **[practice; Baylor specifics via secondary summaries — primary PDF unfetchable]** [Baylor resources](https://library.web.baylor.edu/visit/institute-oral-history/resources), [Margaret Walker Center guide](https://www.jsums.edu/margaretwalkercenter/files/2024/10/Margaret-Walker-Center-OH-Transcription-Style-Guide.pdf)
- **Narrator review is a standard pipeline stage, not a courtesy.** The foundational text *Transcribing and Editing Oral History* (Baum, AASLH) gives "The Narrator's Review and Approval" its own chapter; institutional guidance (NPS, Montana State, U Pitt) makes narrator correction-and-release near-universal before a transcript is finalized. **[practice]** [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/oral-history-resources-considering-legal-and-ethical-issues.htm), [Montana State](https://guides.lib.montana.edu/oral_history/release_forms)
- **"Co-created document" is the field's explicit frame.** Columbia OHMA defines oral history as "co-creating dialogic, critical conversations," and frames transcript editing as "the ethics of writing with other people's words." Columbia CCOHR's actual pipeline: vendor transcription, then in-house **audit-edit** (a verification pass against the audio). **[practice]** [Columbia OHMA](http://oralhistory.columbia.edu/why-ohma), [CCOHR FAQs](https://www.ccohr.incite.columbia.edu/faqs)
- **The archive-the-raw alternative**: the Library of Congress Veterans History Project sidesteps transcript-fidelity entirely by requiring deposit of the **unedited original recording** as the primary record. Keeping the raw source available is itself a provenance strategy. **[practice]** [LOC VHP](https://www.loc.gov/programs/veterans-history-project/about-this-program/)
- **StoryCorps' broadcast model**: aggressive curatorial cutting (40 min → 2–8 min) is licensed by **storyteller approval of the edited segment** plus a truthfulness-to-overall-tone norm. Heavy editing + subject sign-off is an accepted pattern. **[practice]** [Firesteel guidelines](http://firesteelwa.org/tips-and-guidelines-for-editing-storycorps-finding-our-way-interviews/)
- Caveat: the Oral History Association's own published Principles are surprisingly light on transcription mechanics — the field's granular norms live in repository style guides, not one central standard. OHA convened a 2024 members-only symposium on AI in oral history; content is gated, so the field's institutional AI position is not publicly retrievable. **[flagged gap]** [oralhistory.org/ai](https://oralhistory.org/ai/). A 2025 editorial addresses informed consent for AI transcription of qualitative interviews (participants should know their words pass through an ASR system, with misinterpretation risk). **[peer-reviewed guidance]** [PMC12048736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12048736/)

### 3.3 Journalism quote norms

- **The hardest line: AP — "Never alter quotations even to correct minor grammatical errors or word usage."** Outlets fall back to paraphrase or partial quotes rather than "fixing" a quote. **[practice; AP entry via secondary summary — subscriber wall]** [Purdue OWL](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/journalism_and_journalistic_writing/ap_style.html), [News Literacy Matters](https://newsliteracymatters.com/2019/10/23/q-should-a-journalist-use-direct-quotations-with-someone-whose-english-is-poor-or-should-they-clean-up-the-quotes/)
- **Quote approval is treated as a corruption of provenance in the other direction**: the NYT banned pre-publication quote approval in 2012 (Abramson: it "puts so much control over the content of journalism in the wrong place"). Reuters permits reading quotes back to *verify* but forbids letting the source materially alter them, and never submits stories for pre-publication vetting. **[practice]** [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2012/the-new-york-times-bans-quote-approval/), [Reuters Handbook (mirror)](https://www.mediareform.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Reuters_Handbook_of_Journalism.pdf). Interesting asymmetry with oral history: journalism *rejects* subject sign-off (independence), oral history *requires* it (co-creation). The boxholder's practice is the oral-history case — the subject IS the author, so approval isn't a corruption, it's the point.
- **PBS's editing rules are the most concrete "arrangement ethics" found** (primary source): editing must never alter meaning; **permitted** — combining statements from different parts of an interview if it fairly represents the remarks, with edits signaled; **forbidden** — making an answer appear to respond to a different question, assembling a composite "quote" never actually spoken by rearranging fragments, conflating interviews, adding pauses. Decision test: does the edit manipulate understanding? **[practice, primary]** [PBS Standards](https://www.pbs.org/standards/blogs/standards-memos/handling-quotations/). This is a ready-made rule set for an AI that arranges a user's words.
- **"Edited and condensed for clarity"** — the standard Q&A disclosure — licenses cutting filler/tangents and reordering for flow *without per-omission ellipses*, but not distorting meaning; sharing the edited draft with the interviewee is considered good practice in this genre (unlike news quotes — different genres, different norms). **[practice]** [The Open Notebook](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2025/07/29/crafting-clear-and-conversational-qas/), [NPR Training](https://training.npr.org/2019/11/12/deciding-what-parts-of-an-interview-to-cut-here-are-some-guidelines/)
- **AI-era newsroom policy** absorbs AI into the existing accuracy framework rather than inventing a new one: bans on AI-invented sources/quotes, same fact-checking bar for AI-assisted copy. LLMs are documented fabricators of quotes (incl. a Wyoming reporter's published AI-fabricated quotes — secondhand via Nieman Lab). **[practice / reported incident]** [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/08/the-good-the-bad-and-the-completely-made-up-newsrooms-on-wrestling-accurate-answers-out-of-ai/), [Poynter AI ethics kit](https://www.poynter.org/ethics-trust/2025/a-lot-has-changed-since-we-created-ai-ethics-guidelines-for-newsrooms-heres-what-you-need-to-know-now/), ["There's a Rule Book in my Head" (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21670811.2025.2495693)
- Fabrication scandals (Cooke, Glass, Blair) are the boundary cases the norms exist to prevent — invented sources/quotes wholesale, a different category from cleanup. **[historical]** [CJR on Glass](https://www.cjr.org/the_feature/the_fabulist_who_changed_journalism.php)

## 4. Editing theory: the profession's bright line between editing and authoring

This is the most directly load-bearing material found for "structure without adding words."

- Standard four-tier taxonomy (CIEP/Chicago-aligned): developmental/structural → line editing → copyediting → proofreading. **[practice]** [CIEP](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/about-proofreading-and-editing.html)
- **Thesis editing is the one place the line is ethics-board-enforced**, because a thesis must remain provably the student's — exactly the property the boxholder wants. The Australian **IPEd "Guidelines for editing research theses"** (endorsed by the Australian Council of Graduate Research; aligned with IPEd Standards 3rd ed. 2024):
  - Professional editing of a thesis is limited to **copyediting and proofreading only**; substantive/structural editing is off-limits.
  - The operative mechanism: **"In relation to matters of substance and structure, the professional editor may draw attention to problems, but should not provide solutions. Examples may be offered in order to guide the student in resolving problems."** (direct quote)
  - The prohibition names the boundary precisely: substantive editing "interferes with the content... or interferes with the language to such an extent that it **constitutes rewriting**." **[practice, primary]** [Guidelines PDF](https://www.iped-editors.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Guidelines-for-Editing-Research-Theses.pdf), [IPEd](https://www.iped-editors.org/about-editing/academic-editing/)
- Universities are extending the same rule verbatim to AI: e.g. Auckland — "Third parties or GenAI must not write or rewrite any part of the thesis... [and] must make no contribution to the intellectual content... other than those disclosed and justified." **[practice]** [Auckland policy](https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/about-us/about-the-university/policy-hub/research-innovation/conduct/third-party-editing-proofreading-theses-dissertations-guidelines.html)
- Caveat: the UK CIEP's own code was not verified to contain an equivalently blunt "must not" clause — the Australian material is the rigorous source here. **[flagged]**

**Design translation:** "point at problems, offer examples, never supply the text" is a ready-made rule for the AI's unsolicited behavior; supplying text happens only as edit-on-instruction, which is the thesis-guideline equivalent of the student making the change themselves after the editor flags it — with the wrinkle that here the "editor" executes the instruction, so the instruction (and its provenance) is what keeps authorship with the human.

## 5. Granular attribution and provenance formats

**Headline: no adopted standard exists for span-level "which words are whose" in text. Everything granular is prototype; everything adopted is document-level.**

### Adopted, document-level
- **C2PA / Content Credentials** (fetched spec, v2.4): manifests with signed assertions, `digitalSourceType` values incl. `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` and `compositedWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`, `c2pa.actions` edit histories, `c2pa.ingredient` chains across editing steps. v2.4 added embedding manifests in plain text (via non-rendering Unicode variation selectors) and structured text (Markdown, source code, YAML). **But all text bindings are whole-file data-hashes — there is no span-level assertion primitive.** **[standard]** [C2PA 2.4 spec](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html), [IPTC digitalSourceType](https://iptc.org/news/new-digital-source-type-term-added-to-support-inpainting-outpainting-in-generative-ai/). Independent critique: C2PA "cannot effectively represent scenarios where only portions of media undergo alteration." **[empirical critique]** [arXiv:2604.24890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24890)
- **Academic publishing policies** (ICMJE, COPE, Nature, Science, IEEE, arXiv) are unanimous on two points: **(a) AI cannot be an author because authorship = accountability** (can't consent, defend integrity, hold copyright, be sued); **(b) disclosure is required only at methods/acknowledgments granularity** — no policy requires or defines per-passage marking. IEEE explicitly exempts grammar/editing-level AI use from disclosure. **[standard]** [ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html), [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-00191-1), [Science](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/change-policy-use-generative-ai-and-large-language-models), [arXiv policy](https://blog.arxiv.org/2023/01/31/arxiv-announces-new-policy-on-chatgpt-and-similar-tools/)
- **ACL 2023 is the most granular adopted taxonomy of assistance levels** — and maps almost directly onto the boxholder's design space: (1) predictive/autocomplete input assistance — no disclosure; (2) literature search — no disclosure if verified; (3) **"assistance purely with the language of the paper"** (paraphrasing/polishing the author's existing content) — allowed, disclosure encouraged; (4) **content + new-idea generation** — analogized to undisclosed co-authorship, discouraged. **[standard]** [ACL 2023 policy](https://2023.aclweb.org/blog/ACL-2023-policy/). The elicit/arrange/edit-on-instruction practice sits in category 3 territory by design; the point of the process is to make category 4 structurally impossible.
- Adoption reality check: only ~2.5% of published articles include any AI-disclosure statement at all. **[empirical]** [DAISY paper, arXiv:2604.02760](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02760v1)

### Proposed / prototyped span-level (none adopted)
- **Faceted span-level attribution schema** (Xexéo, 2026): tag text segments by facet (ideation, drafting, editing/refinement, citation, language enhancement) in RDF-style metadata; explicitly positions C2PA + journal norms as the coarse status quo. **[proposal]** [arXiv:2604.25346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25346)
- **DraftMarks** (2025): inline skeuomorphic provenance in the final document — masking tape over AI-generated passages, smudge marks for tone-only AI edits, ghost-text hover revealing discarded generations and prompts. N=70 evaluation: ~50% better reader comprehension of the writing process vs. baseline disclosure. The most developed *presentation* design for span provenance. **[proposal + empirical eval]** [arXiv:2509.23505](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23505v1)
- **OpAI-Bench** (2026): benchmark with provenance preserved at document/sentence/token/span granularity across five edit-operation types (polish, paraphrase, expand, compress…) — treats span attribution as a localization/eval problem. Its edit-operation taxonomy is directly reusable as a provenance vocabulary. **[proposal/benchmark]** [arXiv:2606.06481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06481)
- **CoAuthor** (Stanford): keystroke-level logs of 1,445 human–GPT-3 sessions, 13 event types, public replay interface — the research-grade template for *process capture* as provenance. **[empirical dataset]** [coauthor.stanford.edu](https://coauthor.stanford.edu/)
- **AnchoredAI**: anchoring AI comments to the relevant text span (vs. detached chat) improved writer agency and reduced overreliance. **[proposal + eval, preprint]** [arXiv:2509.16128](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16128)
- Document-level disclosure card schemes: **AI Usage Cards** (machine-readable per-phase disclosure) [ai-cards.org](https://ai-cards.org/); **IBM AI Attribution Toolkit** (CRediT-inspired questionnaire → CC-style attribution statement) [IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/AI-attribution-toolkit); **DAISY** (six categories × none/low/medium/high) [arXiv:2604.02760](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02760v1); **GAIDeT** (stage × role declaration taxonomy) [Leiden Madtrics](https://www.leidenmadtrics.nl/articles/gaidet-a-practical-taxonomy-for-declaring-ai-use-in-research-and-publishing); tool/assistant/collaborator/autonomous-discoverer levels [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=siD2eKSsYy). All document-level despite "granular" branding. **[proposals]**

### Shipped and near-span-level: exactly one product
- **Grammarly Authorship**: live process capture (keystrokes + paste events) classifying each part of a document as human-typed / pasted / AI-generated / AI-assisted-grammar. Genuinely near span-level — but it's process capture (you must have run it while writing), not a portable verifiable format; interoperates with nothing. **[shipped product]** [grammarly.com/authorship](https://www.grammarly.com/authorship)
- **iA Writer Authorship** (see §6) is the other shipped instance — span-level authorship marking maintained *in the editor*, fully local.
- Adjacent informal practice: Draftback-style Google Docs history replay as ad-hoc evidence in AI-cheating disputes. **[informal practice]** [draftback.com](https://draftback.com/)

### What doesn't work
- **Watermarking** answers "did a model produce this stretch," fragilely — light paraphrase or human editing destroys token-level signal; it cannot do mixed-document span attribution. **[empirical]** [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WbFhFvjjKj), [arXiv:2605.25796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25796)
- **Detection** is unreliable on hybrid text: commercial AI detectors 61–69% accurate, worse on human-edited AI text and non-native writers; keystroke-timing verification is attackable. Process-based provenance (capture at write time) is the defensible approach — which is what the boxholder's design produces natively. **[empirical]** [arXiv:2406.15335](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15335v1), [arXiv:2601.17280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17280)

### Coarse labels are already recognized as too coarse
"Not By AI" badge (≥90% human threshold), Books By People "Organic Literature" certification — the market inventing percentage/scope schemes because binary human/AI labeling fails. **[opinion/industry movement]** [notbyai.fyi](https://notbyai.fyi/about), critique: "Nothing is 100% human authored" [LSE Impact Blog](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/impactofsocialsciences/2026/04/27/nothing-is-100-human-authored/)

## 6. Tools and philosophies that assemble rather than generate

- **iA Writer Authorship — the closest existing precedent to the boxholder's provenance goal.** Span-level authorship tracking in the editor: your words in default text; AI-originated text visually marked (initially dimmed grey, revised to a more visible multi-author scheme because grey "was too quiet"); other humans' text underlined; all local, nothing sent to a server. Stated philosophy: "Own what you write"; use AI "to point out flaws in your writing instead of having it write for you"; after importing external text, "rewrite the text until it accurately reflects your voice." **[shipped product + manifesto]** [ia.net: Authorship in Times of AI](https://ia.net/topics/ia-writer-7), [See What AI Wrote](https://ia.net/topics/see-what-ai-wrote), [how-to](https://ia.net/writer/how-to/track-authors-and-ai), [third-party analysis](https://cartography.pika.page/posts/dont-lose-yourself-to-the-goblin-using-ia-writers-authorship-to-manage-your-ai-edits)
- **Lex**: "co-pilot, not autopilot" — restraint in generating, but no provenance tracking. **[product philosophy]** [lex.page/about](https://lex.page/about)
- **Memoir products form a useful contrast pair.** **Storyworth**: word-for-word transcription, no AI rewriting, "sounds exactly like the person" — preservation without assembly. **Remento**: "Speech-to-Story" AI that "polishes and transforms... rewrites the spoken word into a story" — assembly that crosses into generation — but keeps a QR link to the original recording as provenance. The boxholder's design sits between them: more arrangement than Storyworth, no rewriting like Remento. **[product marketing]** [Storyworth/Remento comparison](https://welcome.storyworth.com/blog/remento---how-it-works-pricing-alternatives), [Remento review](https://evermemory.ai/en/blog/remento-review-2026)
- **AudioPen**: voice→text with an adjustable **edit-intensity dial** ("how heavy the edit is") — a concrete UI precedent for user-controlled distance-from-verbatim; note it rewrites by default ("so you sound perfect"), i.e. leans generation. **[product, one-line per brief]** [audiopen.ai](https://www.audiopen.ai/)
- **Zettelkasten/Ahrens grounding**: rephrasing in your own words is what produces understanding — "the attempt to rephrase an argument in our own words confronts us without mercy with all the gaps in our understanding." Philosophical backing for why the human must supply the words even when the AI structures them: the own-words act *is* the thinking. Consistent with the MIT self-quoting finding (§2.3). **[opinion/pedagogy, well-established]** [zettelkasten.de](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/concepts-sohnke-ahrens-explained/), [summary](https://aliabdaal.com/book-notes/how-to-take-smart-notes/)

## 7. Elicitation: getting someone's real words out

- **StoryCorps/oral-history craft**: start easy and concrete ("Where were you born?") before hard material; open questions ("How was…?") never leading ones ("Wasn't it…?"); follow-ups — "Tell me more," "Describe what that was like"; the question list is scaffold, not script — "when you hear something that moves you, ask more questions." **[practice]** [StoryCorps guidelines](https://archive.storycorps.org/question-lists/some-guidelines-on-how-to-conduct-a-good-oral-history-interview/)
- **Journalism craft** (CJR roundtable — Jodi Kantor, Max Linsky, Gina Delvac): specificity born of homework unlocks better answers than generic prompts; prepare 20 questions, ask ~10 ("if you need to ask all 20, you're not having a conversation"); save the hardest questions for last, after trust; **silence is a tool** — letting a question float a beat too long yields more genuine answers; and stay alert enough to notice the quotable moment when it happens. **[practitioner craft]** [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/realtalk/the_art_of_the_interview.php)
- **Motivational interviewing (OARS)** — open questions, affirmations, reflective listening, summarizing — is the most rigorously developed elicitation framework found; clinically built precisely to get a person to voice their own reasoning in their own words rather than be led. Reflective listening + summarizing map directly to an AI that plays back the user's words for confirmation. **[practice, adjacent field]** [OARS overview](https://homelesshub.ca/resource/motivational-interviewing-open-questions-affirmation-reflective-listening-and-summary-reflections-oars/)
- **Question design evidence from the one question-only-AI study**: concrete sensory/memory questions anchored student elaboration better than abstract reflective questions ("When AI only asks," §2.4). **[empirical, small-N]**
- Disability-journalism norm as negative-space principle: never accept a proxy's paraphrase of someone's experience — a direct analog for "the AI must not paraphrase where it can elicit." **[practice]** [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/the_feature/journalism-disability-beat.php)
- Moehringer on the precondition for real material: sustained trust — "That's how you know you can trust an author — if he's willing to get raw." **[opinion]** [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/tennis/news-andre-agassi-suffering-intense-ache-wanted-tell-story-know-how-open-prince-harry-s-spare-ghostwriter)

## 8. Failure modes: how the voice gets lost

Collated across fields:

1. **Draft exposure flips the writer into evaluator mode** — they react to text instead of generating it, and absorb the AI's framing/topics/opinions without noticing (Jakesch; Reactive Writers). **[empirical]**
2. **Delegation drift** — assistance ratchets toward "only minor adjustments," with felt loss of agency ("I am not thinking anymore, just following the path"). **[empirical]**
3. **Homogenization** — tuned models pull everyone toward the same vocabulary, content, and (for non-Western writers) culture (Padmakumar & He; Doshi & Hauser; Agarwal). **[empirical]**
4. **Curation betrayal** — selection/arrangement alone can falsify a person even with zero invented words (Schwartz/Trump; PBS's forbidden composite-quote edits). Provenance of words doesn't secure provenance of *meaning*. **[documented practice]**
5. **The ghostwriter effect** — low felt ownership coexisting with public self-declared authorship; and conversely, "add the egg" — trivial effort manufacturing felt ownership without real authorship. Felt ownership is a bad proxy; process is the ground truth. **[empirical]**
6. **Memory/identity loss** — people can't quote their own AI-assisted text minutes later (MIT preprint). **[empirical, preprint]**
7. **Transcription smoothing as falsification** — cleaning speech beyond intelligent-verbatim can misrepresent the speaker as much as raw verbatim misrepresents a fluent one; oral history's answer is explicit conventions + narrator review + keeping the raw recording. **[practice]**
8. **AI drafts poison elicitation** — starting from a generated draft bypasses the interview where the authentic, unexpected material surfaces (Bernoff). **[opinion]**

## 9. Honest gaps

- **No direct experiment tests "AI asks questions" vs. "AI provides draft" on voice/ownership outcomes.** The hypothesis rests on converging indirect evidence (§2.4). This is a genuine open research question — arguably one the boxholder's practice could generate data on.
- **No adopted span-level text-provenance format exists** — the design will have to invent its own markup; DraftMarks (presentation), OpAI-Bench's edit-operation taxonomy (vocabulary), CoAuthor (event log granularity), and oral history's bracket conventions are the best available raw material.
- **Ghostwriters on AI** is one practitioner essay deep. **Oral history's institutional AI position** is member-gated. **CIEP's (UK) editing-vs-authorship ethics clause** unverified; use IPEd (Australia). **Baylor style guide** specifics rest on secondary summaries.
- No forensic-linguistic study measures voice-fidelity of ghostwritten memoirs against subjects' attested speech — an open empirical gap.
- The "pro quote-approval" side of the journalism debate is thinly represented in retrievable sources.

## 10. Design implications (synthesis)

1. **Never show an unrequested draft.** The strongest empirical thread (latent opinion shift, reactive-writing mode-flip, design fixation, homogenization) all triggers on *exposure to generated candidate text*. Elicit first; the thesis-editing rule operationalizes the boundary: **flag problems and offer examples, never supply the prose.**
2. **Capture provenance at write time, as process events, not detection after the fact.** Detection and watermarking demonstrably fail on hybrid text; Grammarly Authorship, iA Writer, and CoAuthor show process capture works. The elicitation transcript is the raw recording — keep it, LOC-VHP-style, as the verifiable source layer.
3. **A three-way span taxonomy is defensible and has precedent**: verbatim (the user's words as spoken/typed), edited-on-instruction (user's words, user-directed transformation — record the instruction as part of provenance), and editorial insertion (connective tissue — mark it, oral-history-bracket-style, and minimize it). OpAI-Bench's operation types (polish/paraphrase/expand/compress) can label the edit kind.
4. **Arrangement needs rules of its own** — PBS's list transfers directly: never make an answer appear to address a different question; no composite sentences stitched from fragments the user never said together; edits signaled. The Schwartz case shows selection can falsify with zero invented words — so the user must review and approve the *arrangement*, not just the words (oral history's narrator-review stage; StoryCorps' approval-of-the-cut).
5. **Distance-from-verbatim should be an explicit, user-controlled dial** (full verbatim → intelligent verbatim → edited), following the transcription industry's spectrum and AudioPen's edit-intensity control; preserve meaningful false starts, drop pure filler by default.
6. **Elicitation design**: concrete/sensory/memory questions over abstract ones (the one empirical result on question type); open never leading; reflective playback of the user's own words (OARS); easy-to-hard sequencing; tolerate silence; prepared questions as scaffold not script.
7. **Don't trust felt ownership as a success metric** — it's manufacturable ("add the egg") and dissociable from real contribution (ghostwriter effect). Measure process facts: fraction of final words traceable to user utterances, edit-instruction coverage, self-quoting ability.
8. **Naming**: no term exists to adopt; "as told to" is the nearest cultural anchor, ACL's "assistance purely with the language" the nearest policy category, and the accountability rationale (ICMJE/COPE: the author is whoever can stand behind the words) the strongest principled justification for the human-authorship claim the practice wants to make.
