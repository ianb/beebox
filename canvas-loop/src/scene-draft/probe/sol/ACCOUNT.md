# Stage 2 drawer account — sol

I wrote a market row, a panning night room, and a pendulum figure. All three are drawings with actual shapes and composition, but this remains a paper probe: no scene renderer exists, so I cannot say whether the resulting pictures read well. The requested TypeScript check prints no diagnostics for this folder.

The familiar calls came quickly. I used `v.rect`, `v.path`, `v.curve`, `v.text`, and `v.at` without referring back to the proposal. A shared `drawVendor(v, apron, skin)` was a natural component, and `v.picture({ key: "market-vendor", width: 78, height: 106, deps: [vendor.apron, vendor.skin] }, ...)` made the color variants explicit. For the room, putting `v.at({ translate: [-cameraX, 0] }, ...)` outside the watercolor group followed the draft's camera rule and kept the wash in world coordinates. `textBounds` gave the pendulum label a box sized to its actual text.

I reached for a way to express a live slider value in the prescribed two-argument `draw(v, model)` form. I declared `params.length`, placed `v.control("length", ...)`, and put `length` in `Model`. That assumes the host's `update` copies param changes into the model; this sketch does not supply that update. The draft says controls remain as today but does not show that bridge in the scene examples. I also wanted a concise built-in trail operation; instead `Model.pastAngles` holds history and `drawTrail` paints the points. That keeps the drawing deterministic, but leaves history maintenance to an update function outside this probe.

The most uncertain line is the market's `v.group({ name: "vendor lantern", effects: { glow: ... } }, ...)` inside a lit group. I guessed that the glow effect adds light-colored pixels while the separately declared `v.light(...)` actually illuminates nearby stalls and people. The contract calls glow pixels emissive, yet lighting is applied before group effects, so I am unsure whether this nested glow receives any light treatment or only becomes a visible halo at the final composite. In the night room I guessed that `ambient: "#30374a"` means dim blue illumination, though the relationship between the ambient color's brightness and light `energy` is not specified numerically.

The picture `deps` rule was easy to follow for colors and intentionally kept frame-dependent breathing outside the picture. I set paper again inside the vendor picture because the draft says a picture does not inherit the placing scope's paper. That is clear, but it is an easy omission when composing a painted scene. I believe the effects order is wash then paper on the lit room; I did not need another nesting level to reverse it. The room's furniture shadow effect and `castsShadow` material express different shadows; I expect both, but without pixels I cannot judge whether they look doubled. I did not use a group clip or mask, so their isolation rules remained untested here.

Compared with Canvas2D or p5, the scene API removes manual save/restore and makes reuse, lighting, and painterly treatment much shorter to write. Compared with SVG, it lets me describe brush strokes and light in the same drawing vocabulary. The cost is less direct control over compositing and uncertainty about which layer an effect reads. I would add a small, complete scene example showing a lit emissive lamp with a glow, and one showing `params` plus `update` plus an over-canvas slider. Those would settle the two guesses that most affected these sketches.
