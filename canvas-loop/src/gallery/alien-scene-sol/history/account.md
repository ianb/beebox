# Drawing account

I drew a traveler on a dusk meadow, waving to a friend in a little ship. The guide supplied the alien's recognizable oval head, yellow-tipped antennae, large eyes, smile, pear body, and wave. I chose the scarf, satchel, moon, layered hills, and flowers to give the departure a place and a mood.

## What fought me

The first render's long bristle marks across the sky made strong, evenly spaced stripes. I expected loose atmospheric color, but the repeated curves read as wallpaper. Grouping those strokes at 25 percent opacity, spacing them farther apart, and adding a few shorter cloud marks let the gradient show through. The head's bristle strokes also look more like deliberate stripes than subtle modeling when zoomed in, though they give the face some painted character at page scale. I found no library bug.

The raised hand was harder to articulate at this size. I added two small finger lines, expecting them to clarify the wave. The hand zoom showed they barely register; it still looks like a mitten. The pose and the tiny passenger waving back make the exchange legible at full size.

## What I wanted and did not have

I wanted a way to shape a brush mark's internal color and edge with the same ease as its outline. Fills are flat, so I built color with clipped or overlaid strokes, which took several layers to keep the scene from looking cut out. I also wanted the distant land to show richer broken paint texture without competing with the alien; the final hills only approach that.

## Brushes

I used bristle for the sky, skin, scarf highlight, land, grass, and flowers; its streaks give the page most of its paint character. Ink gave me the varied dark contours, smile, and antennae. Pencil supplied fine grass and hand marks. Chalk gave the moon edge and ship trail a broken grain. Wash softened the moon and face highlight, and airbrush made the moon and ship glow. Bristle disappointed me in the first sky pass: many similar long strokes made a mechanical pattern. Pencil disappointed me on the hand because its thin marks vanished at page scale.

## What the crops changed

The first face crop showed the skin strokes clearly and reassured me that they were brush marks rather than accidental flat shading. The ship crop made me add a raised arm to the passenger; it now reads as a reply to the main character. The final hand crop corrected my optimism about the finger marks: they did not solve the mitten shape. The hill crop showed the new muted brush layers were visible but appropriately quiet.

## How it feels

The process was quick and absorbing because every render gave a concrete judgment to make. I like the warm exchange across the cool dusk and the small travel details. The result feels like a complete picture-book moment, though the hand could be better drawn, the hills could carry more varied paint, and the ship is still simpler than the main character.
