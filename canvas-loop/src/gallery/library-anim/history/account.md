# The Library That Crossed the Flood, Awake

I kept the painted crossing as the page's anchor. The turtle blinks once in the short loop; the water carries a few moving reflections, the lantern breathes softly, and four fireflies wander near the shell. A reader can hover over the lantern to draw its light closer, click it to dim or relight the books, and touch or drag across the foreground water to leave widening ripples. Controls change the current's pace, switch the lantern, and hide the fireflies. These are small acts suited to a quiet rescue crossing rather than a game laid over it.

The expensive painting is drawn once into `v.cache("painted-library", ...)`. Each later frame draws only plain lines, ellipses, a small gradient, and the temporary ripple rings. In the warmed headless comparison, the one-frame command took 2.08 seconds and the 61-frame command took 2.34 seconds with `--every 1000`; the difference is about **4.3 ms per additional frame**. A previous pair gave about 5.7 ms, so I would describe the headless cost as roughly **4–6 ms per cached frame**, well below the 30 ms aim. This is a CLI measurement, not a browser frame-time measurement. Frames with many saved snapshots take longer overall because writing PNGs is part of the run.

The first moving water marks looked like mechanical rows of dashes. Fewer, fainter marks let the original bristle reflections show through. The first light pass washed out the books, so I reduced the window pulse. The original page already contains a lantern halo and a bright room; with the whole painting cached, turning the lantern off needs a darkening pass over the window, and a trace of the original halo remains. That is the most visible compromise. The copied drawing also emits faint-brush warnings inherited from the still page. TEA's import rule required the painting to live in the `*-tea.ts` file rather than a local helper; its readonly model typing needed care when adding ripples. The scripted mouseup after a lantern click is correctly unhandled because the lantern acts on mousedown; the water release is handled.

I wanted a way to cache the turtle, room, and background as separate painted layers and move a layer without the cache retaining its first transform. That would let the turtle bob through the flood and would make a true lantern-off state easier. I kept the body still and gave it a blink instead. The result feels like a living illustration without pulling the eye away from the keeper and the books. The water rings are more geometric than the original brushwork, but they read clearly as a reader's touch and fade quickly.

Four render cycles were used. The final transcript shows named engagement for lantern hover and toggle, water touch, drag, and release; pinned frames show the dimmed window, ripples, and blink. ESLint passes. The package typecheck reported no errors in `library-anim`, but it currently fails in the concurrently edited `nell-anim` and `pell-anim` galleries.
