# alien-scene-opus-b — cycle notes

Scene: "Hello, Earth!" A green alien has just come down the ramp of its landed saucer onto a dusk meadow and waves at the reader. 800×600.

## Cycle 1
**Intent:** First full pass, not a block-in: dusk gradient sky with wash cloud bands, moon with airbrush glow, stars; three hill layers (wash and chalk texture inside clips); saucer with dome, windows, ramp and a light beam; meadow painted with bristle bands, ink grass tufts, flowers; alien built from `tube()` limbs plus curve head/body, wash shading inside clips, warm rim light on the right from the saucer, ink outlines; fireflies with airbrush glow.
**Seen:** Composition reads at once: moon top-left, alien left of centre, saucer right, dusk gradient behind. Many faults. (1) The alien looks flat: the green-dark wash at opacity 0.55 is almost invisible on the head and body, the warm rim on the right edge is invisible, and the airbrush cheeks do not show at all. (2) The chalk belly ring reads as a big "0" printed on the belly. (3) Draw order is wrong: limb outlines cross the body (the down arm's shoulder line, the leg tops), and the body's top outline shows under the mouth as a second "chin" line. (4) The meadow bristle bands start at random x, so their square starts make a staircase of hard-edged blocks (zoom 2 shows this plainly). (5) The ramp is a flat grey wedge that does not touch anything and the light cone is a hard polygon — clip-art. (6) Fireflies are invisible. The mouth wobble makes a "v", not a smile. Good: sky gradient, stars, moon, the chalk texture on the middle hills, ink line weight on the outlines.

## Cycle 2
**Intent:** Fix structure and make the light visible. Draw order: limbs (fill+shade+outline) first, then body, then head over it, so joins hide. Replace belly ring with a soft wash patch. Shading much stronger (multiply-blended wash in a deep green, opacity ~0.8) and the warm rim with an ink/wash stroke in "screen" so it shows. Cheeks as small filled pink ellipses softened with airbrush. Meadow bands start and end offscreen. Remove the ramp; replace the hard light cone with a group of soft wash strokes (screen). Fireflies bigger glow. Smile without wobble.
**Seen:** Clear gain. Limb joins are hidden now, the chin line is gone, multiply wash gives visible form shadow down the body's left side and under the head with a mottled paper texture (zoom 2), the cheeks show, the three-finger hand reads as a wave, and the warm rim highlight shows on the waving arm. The beam is now soft instead of a hard wedge. Still wrong: (1) the belly wash is a vertical grey-green smear — it reads as a stain, not a tummy patch. (2) The body's warm rim on the right edge does not show at all. (3) The saucer has legs that end in mid-air against the hills; it is hovering above the ground, so legs make no sense. Its dome outline is a broken stroke that cuts across the dome. The disc is flat grey. (4) The beam is grey-green, not warm, and stops without lighting the ground. (5) The meadow's bristle bands read as horizontal stripes with small stair-steps at the ends (zoom 3), not as grass. (6) The ground shadow under the alien is too faint to anchor him.

## Cycle 3
**Intent:** Make the saucer hover on purpose (the alien was just beamed down): remove legs, add a dark underside, a bright rim highlight, a correct dome outline, a warmer beam and a glowing pool of light on the grass where it lands. Belly: a lighter tummy oval (screen wash) instead of a dark smear. Body rim light as a narrower, stronger screen stroke. Meadow: replace horizontal bands with soft wash colour variation plus many short upward bristle strokes of grass, larger in the foreground. Stronger contact shadow.
**Seen:** Big improvement in the ground and saucer. The meadow is now grass: tapering ink blades, longer and thicker toward the front, with soft wash value bands under them (zoom 3), and the lit blades near the beam turn yellow. The saucer hovers believably with a dark underside, a lit hatch, a white rim highlight, and a clean dome outline (zoom 2). The tummy is a soft lighter patch now, no stain. Contact shadow is visible. Still wrong: (1) the light pool on the grass is a radial gradient inside an ellipse; the gradient is circular but the ellipse is wide and flat, so its top and side edges are cut and a hard oval edge shows from x≈400 to 800. (2) The beam is grey-white haze over the blue hill; it reads as fog more than warm light. (3) The alien looks pasted on: a bright, saturated sticker-green against a dusk scene, with green-grey shadows that do not pick up the purple sky. The feet sit on top of the grass with no blades in front of them. (4) The two white wiggles beside the hand read as steam, not as a wave.

## Cycle 4
**Intent:** Put the alien into the scene's light: cool violet multiply shadows on the side away from the beam, a slightly deeper base green, a warmer and stronger rim on the beam side. Light pool: replace the clipped gradient ellipse with wide horizontal screen-wash strokes. Beam: warmer colour, plus a few airbrush sparkles in it. Grass blades in front of the feet after the alien is drawn. Replace the wiggles with three short arc ticks around the hand.
**Seen:** Mixed. Helped: the blades in front of the feet plant him in the grass (zoom 1); the light pool's hard oval edge is gone and the lit grass now fades softly; the three arc ticks around the hand read as a wave better than the wiggles. Did not help: the alien's shadows are still green — the violet multiply wash darkens the left side a little but I cannot see violet in it; at full frame he still looks like a sticker on a painting. The warm rim on the head's right edge is a thin pale line, visible only in the zoom. The beam is still grey haze; screen-blending orange over the blue-grey hill lightens it toward grey instead of warming it. The beam sparkles do not show at all (airbrush 10 px screen on an already light beam).

## Cycle 5
**Intent:** Tint the whole alien with the scene light in one pass: clip to his silhouette (head+body+arms+legs path pieces combined) and lay a multiply linear gradient, violet on the moon/sky side to warm cream on the beam side. Beam: draw it with normal blend in a translucent warm yellow so it reads as warm light, not haze; sparkles as small bright dots with a glow. Tilt the head about 5° toward the waving hand so the pose is less rigid.
**Seen:** The tint pass did what four shading strokes could not: at full frame the alien now belongs to the dusk — his green is cooler on the left and warmer on the right, and he no longer reads as a sticker. It went slightly too far: he is now an olive, slightly muddy green and has lost some of the character "pop" a picture book would keep for its hero. The head tilt works: he leans toward his waving hand and looks less like a diagram (zoom 1). The cheeks are brown-grey ovals (translucent pink over green makes brown) with hard edges. The beam is now yellow haze instead of grey, a little better; the sparkles show as small white dots (zoom 2). The outline is still one even dark line everywhere.

## Cycle 6
**Intent:** Lighten the tint gradient so he keeps a clear green. Cheeks: opaque pink with an airbrush blush around them. Line variation: a heavier ink accent along the shadow side (lower-left) of head, body and the down arm. Depth and story in the background: a row of dark rounded trees on the left of the middle hill and a tiny cottage with one lit window on the far right hill (someone to wave to); two soft lit clouds near the horizon. Moon gets a faint wash texture.
**Seen:** Mixed again. Helped: the lighter tint gives back a clear green while keeping the cool-left/warm-right light; the cheeks are now pink, not brown; the moon has soft wash dark patches (zoom 2) and stopped looking like a sticker; the row of dark rounded trees gives the left side depth and the tiny cottage with a lit window on the far hill (zoom 3) adds a place in the world. Hurt: (1) the body accent line does not sit on the body's edge — my hand-placed points run 5–10 px outside it, so zoom 1 shows a doubled outline between the lowered arm and the body. (2) The clouds failed: the dark wash part is invisible against the sky, and the pink "lit edge" with wobble 5 is a thin wiggly ribbon that reads as a worm or a contrail, not a cloud (zoom 3, upper). The head accent (heavier lower-left arc) works and is subtle. The cottage's glow airbrush does not show; its hard polygon is acceptable at this size.

## Cycle 7
**Intent:** Body accent: draw it through the BODY points themselves (an open curve through the left-side points) so it lands on the outline. Clouds: replace the strokes with filled bumpy cloud shapes in a muted violet, with a pink wash clipped to their underside and no wobble, so they read as solid soft clouds lit from the sunset below.
**Seen:** Both changes failed, and one diagnosis was wrong. (1) The doubled line on the body's left is still there with the accent now exactly on the body outline (zoom 1). So in cycle 6 I misread it: the second line is the lowered arm's inner edge, which runs parallel to the body a few pixels outside it and leaves a green sliver between two dark lines. The accent was never the cause. (2) The clouds are now smooth flat lenses — the Catmull-Rom curve through my "bump" points rounds them away — and the right one, directly above the saucer, looks like a second flying saucer (zoom 3). That is worse than the worms: it confuses the story.


## Cycle 8
**Intent:** Lowered arm: angle it away from the body (shoulder tucked inside the body, elbow out at x≈256, hand at x≈232) so no parallel sliver remains. Clouds: build each from 4–5 overlapping filled circles (real cumulus bumps) on a flat base, keep the pink wash underside in a clip; move the right cloud left and up so it does not sit over the saucer.
**Seen:** Clouds now read as clouds: bumpy cumulus tops, a flat base, a pink wash underneath (zoom 2), and the right one no longer sits on the saucer. They are crisp flat shapes, a bit clip-art next to the soft sky, but they no longer confuse the story. The lowered arm angles away from the body and reads as an arm. But the doubled line on the body's left is still there, unchanged, even though the arm no longer runs beside it (zoom 1). So my cycle-7 diagnosis was also wrong. Reading the code instead of guessing: the body accent sits *after* `s.push(); translate; rotate(0.08)` for the head tilt, because I inserted it just above the "head over the body" comment, which by then was inside the rotated block. The accent is rotated 4.6° about the neck, so it drifts left of the body edge toward the bottom. Two wrong visual diagnoses in a row; the zooms showed the symptom clearly but not the cause.

## Cycle 9
**Intent:** Move the body accent above the `push()` so it is not rotated. Add sparse pencil hatching (multiply, curved strokes following the form) in the shadow areas of head and body, so the alien's colour has a drawn texture instead of only flat fill plus soft wash. Brighter, narrower core in the beam.
**Seen:** The doubled line is finally gone; the body's left edge now has a single heavier line, which gives the figure a shadow side in the line work itself (zoom 1). The pencil hatching on the body shows as grainy diagonal strokes on the shadow side — at full frame it reads as a drawn texture, subtle; in the zoom it is clearly pencil. The contour hatching on the head is almost invisible: three faint arcs I can only find because I know where they are. The new beam core does not show at all (zoom 2): an airbrush at 0.6 inside a group at 0.55 over a light beam is too weak. No regressions elsewhere.

## Cycle 10
**Intent:** Final pass. Head hatching stronger (higher opacity and weight) so it matches the body. Beam core drawn outside the beam group, normal blend, a brighter cream airbrush, so the column of light has a centre. No other changes, to keep the last render a clean check.
**Seen:** Small gains, both marginal. The head hatching now shows in the zoom as grainy pencil arcs just inside the lower-left outline (zoom 1); at full frame it adds a little darkening there and nothing more. The beam core is a faint lighter vertical stripe in the zoom (zoom 2) and barely visible at 1×; the beam is still a soft haze. Nothing regressed. After this render, lint and typecheck required behaviour-preserving edits (index guards in `tube()`, a `null` shade instead of `() => undefined`, removal of an unused constant); I did not re-render, so `sketch.ts` differs from `cycle-10.txt` only in those lines.
