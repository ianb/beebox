# open-opus-b notes

Book: *Old Pell Lights the Lamps*. An old heron in a red scarf walks the boardwalk out from a stilt village every dusk, lighting the lamps so the boats can find their way home. Page: the last lamp, at the end of the pier, one small boat still out on the water.

## Cycle 1
**Intent:** First full layout: dusk sky with moon and cloud washes, far hills, stilt village at left with lit windows and reflections, water gradient with bristle ripples, a boardwalk receding from lower right to the village, two already-lit lamps behind, the last lamp at the pier's end, and the heron reaching the pole up to it.
**Seen:** (whole frame) The layout reads: moon, village, pier receding, heron at the end. But the heron is too small and thin to carry the page, its body is a narrow sliver under the wing, the pole floats free of the wing (starts below the grip), and the middle lamp post stands directly behind the heron, cluttering its silhouette. (zoom 1) The scarf bristle mark is translucent streaks, reads as a ribbon of gauze. No lamp light falls on the heron. Village reflections are hard flat pentagons. Ripples are faint.

## Cycle 2
**Intent:** Heron at 1.2x with a fuller body, warm rim light on the lamp side (offset crescent inside clip), the pole passing through the wing grip, an opaque knitted scarf (tube fill + pencil rib lines). Move the two lit lamps to x=300 and x=430 so none stands behind the heron. Break the village reflections with horizontal water-coloured strokes. Add the small boat with a light far out on the right.
**Seen:** (whole frame) Heron now holds the page; the lit lamps no longer stand behind it. The boat on the horizon reads. But the head points up into the sky, past the lamp, so Pell is not looking at what Pell is doing. (zoom 1) The rim light is too broad: a flat peach band across the whole back, and the hard shade crescent adds a third edge, so the body reads as stacked cut-outs. The scarf reads as two red pipes with end-cap ellipses where the tubes overlap (each tube outlined separately). The wing grip is a sausage-shaped hand. (zoom 2) The ripple strokes over the village reflections barely show; the reflections are still flat pentagons. (diff highlight) Confirmed where each change landed; the lamp glows show as big blotches in the highlight that are almost invisible in the frame, so the glows are too weak. Bottom third of the page is empty water.

## Cycle 3
**Intent:** Turn the head down toward the lamp (shorter neck, bill angled down). One continuous scarf tube from the front knot round the back into the trailing tail, outlined once. Narrow the rim (smaller offset shift) and soften the shade crescent. Replace the grip with three curled primary feathers. Paint the village reflections as dark horizontal bristle strokes with warm window streaks instead of flipped polygons. Add dark reeds in the foreground corners. Stronger glow for the last lamp and a light pool on the deck under it.
**Seen:** (whole frame) The page now has a light pool on the deck and reeds framing the corners; the painted village reflections and window streaks read as water. (zoom 1) Pell now looks down at the lamp, but the bill tip touches the lamp's arm: a tangent that merges bird and post. The scarf is one band now, much better; the feather "fingers" read as a hand, acceptable for a picture book. The rim on the neck is a clean warm edge. (whole frame) Reeds are thin wiry sticks with no mass. The left half of the water below the village is empty. Transcript: faint-mark warnings for the lowest reflection strokes (alpha 0.25 x bristle flow), expected.

## Cycle 4
**Intent:** Move the last lamp to the very end of the pier (x=740) and raise it, and put Pell's head level with the lantern, bill pointing straight at it with a clear gap. Move the boat to the far right so the post does not cross it. Redraw reeds as filled tapering leaves (tube with profile to 0) with a faint sky rim. Add the moon's reflection as a column of broken horizontal dabs below the moon.
**Seen:** (zoom 1) Bill now points straight at the lantern with a clear gap, and the post stands clear at the pier's end; the gesture reads as "lighting it". The boat sits alone at far right. (whole frame) Reeds have mass now; left clump frames the moon reflection. The moon reflection is a ladder of identical pills, too regular. (diff change crops) change-1 showed the head and lamp move side by side, useful to confirm the gap. Whole picture still looks like flat vector shapes with paper grain: the sky and water carry almost no visible brushwork.

## Cycle 5
**Intent:** Painted-colour pass on sky and water. Sky: visible lavender cloud streaks lit rose on their undersides (wash + bristle), denser near the glow. Water: more and longer bristle strokes in several values, with sky colours mirrored. Moon reflection: irregular, fewer, thinner dabs with gaps and sideways drift.
**Seen:** (zoom 1) Cloud streaks now show as brushed bristle lines with rose undersides; they are thin and a bit evenly spaced but the sky has movement. (whole frame) The water has visible brushwork in several values and reads as water at last; the moon reflection is a drifting broken column instead of a ladder. (diff highlight) Change-1 covered the whole water: almost every pixel moved, which made the highlight useless for judging; the side by side was the better view for a texture pass. The heron and the deck are now the flattest things on the page, which is wrong for the subject.

## Cycle 6
**Intent:** Paint the heron: bristle strokes following the body's form in lighter and darker greys, scalloped covert feather rows on the wing with ink edges, a dark streak line down the front of the neck, pencil hatching for the shadow under the belly. Wood-grain bristle strokes along the deck.
**Seen:** (diff stats) Only 1.3% of pixels changed, mean delta 0.34: most of the work is invisible. (diff change-1, before/after) The body bristle strokes vanished under the wing, which covers almost all of the body; the scallop feathers landed as a lumpy patch in the middle of the wing with little contrast; the pale flight-feather lines show only at the wing's lower edge. (zoom 2) Deck grain barely shows. The neck streak shows as a short dashed line, good at zoom, invisible at 1x. Helped a little, mostly wasted effort.

## Cycle 7
**Intent:** Give the wing a value structure that reads at 1x: pale covert rows along the shoulder with dark scallop edges, a dark band of flight feathers below them, split primary tips at the wing's end; a cool wash shadow over the lower body away from the lamp. Houses: bristle-textured walls with board lines and a sky-lit edge on the roofs, so the village is painted too.
**Seen:** (zoom 1) The wing now reads as a heron's wing: pale covert rows with dark scallop edges over a dark flight-feather band; the split primary tips are small but help the tail end. (zoom 2) Village walls show vertical board texture and the window glows sit on the walls; the roof's lit edge is faint. (diff stats) Again ~1% of pixels: real but local. (whole frame) Pell does not touch the deck: no contact or cast shadow, so the bird floats. The page has no words; as a picture-book page the empty upper-right sky is where the text would sit.

## Cycle 8
**Intent:** Ground Pell: a soft contact shadow under the feet and a cast shadow on the deck falling back-left, away from the lamp. Make the last lamp the brightest point (bigger hot core). Set one line of story text in the upper-right sky, as on a printed page.
**Seen:** (whole frame) The two lines of text in the upper right turn the picture into a page and fill the empty sky with purpose; Georgia italic via the raw ctx looks printed. The last lamp is now clearly the brightest point. (zoom 1, 3x on the feet) The cast shadow does not show at all: a multiply group at 0.5 over a gradient that fades out is too weak on the mid-value deck. The diff change-2 crop was at 1x and too small to judge; the zoom was the view that told me.

## Cycle 9
**Intent:** A cast shadow that shows: darker, higher opacity, clipped to the deck. Vary the outline weight around Pell: heavier on the shadow side (belly, under the tail), thinner on the lamp side, using pressure keyframes along the closed outlines.
**Seen:** (zoom 1) The cast shadow now reads on the deck, falling back-left, and Pell stands on the boards instead of floating. The outline is heavy along the belly and under the tail and thins over the back toward the lamp: the variation shows clearly at zoom and gives the body weight at 1x. (diff stats) 0.7% changed; the highlight located both edits, change-1 confirmed the shadow. Remaining weak spots in the whole frame: the legs are flat yellow strokes, the left cattail heads read as sticks, the roofs' lit edges are faint.

## Cycle 10
**Intent:** Final polish: shade the legs (a darker line along their back edge, a warm touch on the lamp side), make the cattail heads plump brown with a warm rim from the sky glow, and strengthen the lit roof edges.
**Seen:** (zoom 1, 4x) The legs now have a dark back edge and a pale lamp-side line: they read as round, though still like rods more than scaly heron legs. (zoom 2) The cattail heads are brown with a warm stripe, but they come out as square-ended blocks, not the rounded capsules a two-point `tube` should give; at 1x they read as cattails, at zoom as boxes. (diff change-1) The roof edges got brighter; a small change at 1x. (diff stats) 0.6% changed. Budget spent; the page stands as of this cycle.
