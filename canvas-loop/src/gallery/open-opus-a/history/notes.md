# Nell Rows Out — cycle notes

## Cycle 1
**Intent:** First full layout: dusk sky with clouds and evening star, far shore with pines and one lit window, gradient lake with brushed bands, the carp rising under the water (fish then a translucent water layer), lantern reflection, boat with bristle planks, the girl reaching out with the lantern, ripple rings, reeds at the edges.
**Seen:** Whole frame: the layout reads (sky, shore, boat, girl, lantern, reeds) and the dusk palette is pleasant, but the carp reads as a giant eel or worm rising out of the water, not a carp, and it is not submerged at all; the water overlay only made a visible rectangle edge at x=500. The ripple "rings" are big upright arcs in the sky above the water (I used `arc`, which draws circles, not flat water rings). The evening star is a pink disc: my bug, a `noBrush()` without `noStroke()` left the pink 18 px cloud stroke on the glow circle (I wasted a probe sketch finding that out). Zoom 1 (girl): she is a teal blob with a circle head whose outline cuts through the hat; no profile, no neck. The lantern is a flat rectangle and the lantern reflection is a regular ladder of dabs. Zoom 2 (fish): pencil scales are almost invisible. The far shore ends in a hard flat band at the horizon.

## Cycle 2
**Intent:** Rebuild the two weak subjects. Carp: a real carp head (blunt brow, round "O" mouth, large eye, gill cover, barbels) lifting above the waterline toward the lantern, clipped to above-water, with the long body drawn dim under the water in a group. Girl: a profile face with nose and chin, a hat with band and pompom, braid, mustard scarf, arm as a tube, coat clipped with cool shadow and warm lantern side; girl drawn between the boat's inside and its near hull so she sits in it. Ripples as flattened ellipse halves, back half behind the head and front half in front. Fix the star stroke, mirror the far shore into the water, make the lantern reflection irregular, give the lantern a cap and bright core.
**Seen:** Whole frame: much better; it now reads as a girl in a rowboat holding a lantern out to a big fish, and the lantern is the warm focus. Zoom 2 (carp): the head is a boxy loaf or helmet with the mouth on its side, not a fish lifting its snout; the underwater body reads, but the pectoral fin looks like a stray limb. Zoom 1 (girl): profile, hat, mitten and lantern read well; the face's back half is a flat grey-brown block (my shifted shadow shape), so she looks masked, and the scarf reads as a yellow cape on one shoulder. Whole frame: the mirrored shore is a large dark lens under the horizon, and the dark water bands look like floating logs. Diff highlight: everything changed so it mostly confirmed scope, but it did show the hard left edge (x=520) of my deep-water rectangle over the fish body, which the whole frame barely shows.

## Cycle 3
**Intent:** Reshape the carp head so the snout tapers up-left toward the lantern with the round mouth at its tip, the eye looking up, a cheek line and a curved gill cover; drop the stray pectoral fin and sink the body deeper (fainter group, soft elliptical deep-water gradient instead of a rectangle). Compress and fade the shore reflection, soften the dark water bands, halve the glints. Girl: dark hair at the back of the head and an ear instead of the grey shadow block; scarf as a neck band with a tail blowing back.
**Seen:** Zoom 2: the carp now reads as a fish lifting its snout to the lantern, with an upturned eye and a mild smile; it leans a little toward "seal" because the snout is round and the body colour is uniform. Zoom 1: hair at the back of the head fixes the masked look; the scarf tail reads. Whole frame: shore reflection is now a thin soft band, good; the logs are gone. The diff stats and change crops were the useful view this time: change-1 (the horizon band) and change-2 (the fish) were exactly the regions I meant to change, and change-4 (the girl) confirmed the scarf edit without collateral. What the whole frame now shows most: the picture looks like flat vector shapes with grain, not paint, and the left half of the water is empty.

## Cycle 4
**Intent:** Make it painted, not vector: lay long loaded bristle strokes over the sky and water gradients, row by row, each a little lighter or darker than the gradient at its height (a `sample` helper interpolates the stops). Brush the coat along its form (dark back, teal middle, warm lantern edge). Give the carp old koi-like rust patches on its crown so it stops reading as a seal. Add an oar resting in its lock with the blade trailing in the water off the stern, to fill the empty left water and say "she rowed here".
**Seen:** Diff highlight was decisive: the oar, the coat strokes and the koi patches light up, but the 48 bristle strokes I laid over the sky show nothing at all, and the water strokes only a band near the horizon (stats: 2.4% changed). Zoom 1 (sky corner, 2×) shows why: the strokes are there as faint horizontal combing, but ±14 levels at 0.4 opacity is below what the eye picks up at 1×. Whole frame: the oar works; it fills the left water and tells that she rowed out. The koi patch reads as a rust stripe on the crown, acceptable. The underwater body still reads long and eel-like, and the lantern reflection is still a regular ladder.

## Cycle 5
**Intent:** Push the painted strokes until they show: sky and water bristle strokes at ±30 levels and higher opacity, drier loads. Lay an airbrushed mist on the far water for distance. Replace the lantern-reflection ladder with broken, staggered ink glints over a soft wash column. Make the submerged carp fatter, with a dark dorsal fin along its back, so the body says carp and not eel.
**Seen:** Whole frame: the sky and water now look painted, with visible horizontal bristle streaks, and the mist softens the far shore line. But the strokes all start and stop with blunt square ends at a few repeated x positions, so both sky and water show vertical seams, like tiles or plaid (most visible in the whole frame, upper right sky and the water left of the boat). Zoom 2: the new reflection glints are fat lens shapes that read as floating gold coins, not light on ripples; the wash column under them is right. The fatter carp body helps, but its dorsal fin vanishes under the water layer; diff change-3 showed the region changed yet I cannot see the fin in the after crop.

## Cycle 6
**Intent:** Remove the plaid: a `paintRow` helper lays each row as overlapping strokes of varied length, each with a slight curve and a pressure envelope that tapers in and out, starting at a row-specific offset, so no seams line up. Water strokes a touch quieter than the sky. Reflection glints: thin tapered ink dashes instead of lens shapes. Dorsal fin darker so it survives the water layer.
**Seen:** Side by side was the right view for this cycle: the vertical seams on the left are gone on the right, and the sky now reads as layered brushed bands; zoom 1 (upper-right sky, 2×) confirms the tapered ends overlap without tiles. The reflection glints are now thin dashes that read as light on ripples, and the dorsal fin shows as a dark wedge at the right edge under the surface. New: eight "faint mark" warnings, all at x≈790; these are paintRow strokes that start past the right edge so almost nothing lands on canvas. Harmless, but noise to fix. What the frame lacks now: the girl's face has no reaction, and the lower-left water between reeds and oar is still an empty field.

## Cycle 7
**Intent:** Give the girl a reaction: a small open "o" mouth and a raised brow, matching the carp's open mouth. Fill the empty near water with three lily pads (flat dark green, bristle stroke across, the sky caught on the far rim, ink only on the near edge) and one shut bud, as a middle depth layer between the reeds and the boat. Two pale moths at the lantern. Stop paintRow before the right edge to clear the faint-mark warnings.
**Seen:** Zoom 1: the open "o" mouth and raised brow work; she now looks surprised and rapt, which is the page's moment. The moths read only as two pairs of pale specks. Diff change-1 (lily pads, before and after enlarged) was the clearest view of the new layer: the pads are too saturated and too light for dusk water, the notches read as Pac-Man wedges, and the pink bud looks like a candle flame. Whole frame: they do give the lower left a middle depth step, so the idea is right and the colour is wrong. Stats: the faint-mark warnings are gone.

## Cycle 8
**Intent:** Settle the lily pads into the dusk: darker blue-green fill and stroke, a cooler and fainter sky rim, a narrow notch instead of a wedge; the bud becomes a dim mauve closed bud with only its tip catching the sky. Sink the carp's body a little further (fainter group, duller gold) but draw its scales in ink so the pattern still reads through the water. Warm the bow end of the boat's reflection with the lantern.
**Seen:** Zoom 2 (the submerged body, 2×): the ink scales now read through the water, and together with the dorsal fin the shape says "carp" and no longer "eel" or "light beam". But the scales are a rigid lattice of identical arcs on a horizontal grid that ignores the body's diagonal; it looks like wallpaper. Diff change-1 and change-4: the lily pads now sit in the dusk instead of floating above it; the bud is a dim mauve point. Change-3 confirmed the warm patch on the boat reflection, which I would not have spotted in the whole frame.

## Cycle 9
**Intent:** Lay the carp's scales along the body's diagonal axis: staggered rows that widen toward the middle, each arc rotated to open toward the tail and shrinking with depth. Add three lantern-lit drops falling from the carp's chin (it has just come up). Knit ribbing on the hat band in pencil.
**Seen:** The stats said only 0.3% of pixels changed, which made me suspect the scale edit had failed; the diff highlight showed it had not: magenta and green arcs in diagonal rows over the body, plus the hat ribbing as a magenta comb. Zoom 1 (2×): the scales now lie along the body's diagonal and shrink into the deep, and read as a fish swimming up, not wallpaper. Zoom 2: ribbing makes the hat a knit hat; the chin drops are there but tiny. Two new faint-mark warnings at y=799 are scale arcs below the canvas edge. The moths still read as pairs of white dots.

## Cycle 10
**Intent:** Final polish. Moths become two small triangular pale wings around a dark body so they read as moths, not dots. A few faint stars in the dark upper sky. Stop the scale rows before the canvas edge to clear the warnings. A soft multiply vignette toward the corners to hold the eye on the lantern, the girl and the carp.
**Seen:** Whole frame: the vignette is barely perceptible; the diff highlight shows it darkened mainly the two upper corners (the bottom corners sit inside the gradient's flat zone because I centred it low), which is the right amount for a page, but not much hold on the eye. Zoom 1 (4×): the moths are now readable bow-tie moths in the lantern glow; at 1× they are small but no longer ambiguous dots. Faint stars show in the top band. One faint-mark warning remains, for a scale arc that the body clip cuts off at the canvas bottom; it is intended. The picture is done within the budget: girl, lantern and carp read as one moment, with sky, far shore, mid water, boat, pads and reeds as depth layers.
