# Willow Wick

Willow Wick is a one-street animal town with three shops: Bun & Bell, Post & Parcel, and Odd & Ends. There are seventeen clickable residents and things. Every one bobs or breathes at rest, and a hovered target hops under a pale ring and name label. A click starts an act for 72 frames, then the target settles back.

The baker lifts a hot loaf and steam rises; the sweeper brushes the crumbs. The mail carrier scatters letters; the shopkeeper waves; the painter's ladder wobbles. Upstairs, the gardener waters below the sill, the violinist sends notes across the roofs, the tailor unfurls cloth, the librarian lifts a stack of books, and the astronomer watches a small constellation. At street level, the conductor whistles, the cyclist rolls out two wheels, and the skater spins. The bus pulls forward and returns; the rooftop flag flutters, the post-office clock chimes, and the flower box blooms. Five clicks start a neighbor a moment later: the baker cues the sweeper, the violinist cues the skater, the bus cues the conductor, the flag cues the astronomer, and the clock cues the mail carrier.

I painted the buildings, roof tiles, shop displays, notices, chimneys, planters, bus stop, and paper grain into one cached layer, keyed by the dusk setting. Each movable thing has cached idle and active sprites; the frame draw only places and transforms them, then adds a few cheap marks for steam, notes, droplets, and other short-lived effects. In a warmed `time pnpm --dir canvas-loop run cli run ... --every 1000` pair, 121 frames took 1.219 seconds and one frame took 0.891 seconds: the added 120 frames cost about **2.7 ms each**. These wall-clock numbers include CLI startup and one-time painting; the first cold pair was too noisy to subtract meaningfully. The event tour writes many PNGs and takes about ten seconds, so it is not the frame-cost benchmark.

The first render put upstairs people between windows. Moving and scaling them onto the sills made the street much easier to read. The brush texture was pleasing at page scale but is more visible in the zoom. I initially used a six-argument ellipse-style `arc`; this library's `arc` takes five arguments with a radius. Lint also pushed me to split the sprite and effect drawing into smaller helpers. I would have liked a way for hover cues to set the cursor, and a cleaner built-in way to layer an animated person behind a window frame. The current hover ring and sprite placement work, but those two interactions would make the scene feel more natural.

I like the warm, crowded street and the chain reactions. The characters have simple silhouettes and the architecture is quite regular; a future art pass could make the storefronts and species more distinctive. The page is playful and readable now, with a click tour that exercises every target, a visible evening setting, and a reset.

## Revision

I moved hover labels below the street residents or above the upstairs windows, so they no longer cover the shop names. The five upstairs residents now sit behind a final painted window trim. Foxes and cats have pointed ears, mice and bears have round ears, rabbits have long ears, and the frog has raised eyes. The bus and flag click areas fit their visible art, and the bus area follows it when it drives. The flower label sits above its display instead of across the bus. All 17 scripted clicks still have named engagement verdicts.

`--outline` and `v.debugBox` helped: the first outlined frame revealed that the old bus hit box extended into the road, and frame 183 confirmed the repaired box travels with the bus. `v.cursor` now changes to a pointer over a clickable thing and returns to default elsewhere; the headless render cannot show that browser cue. `v.textFont` and `v.textStyle` made the shop signs and hover labels clearer. `blendMode("screen")` made the dusk lamp pools brighter. `--timing` gave direct frame measurements. The new faint-mark warnings reported none; I did not mark any stroke intentionally faint. I did not use `u.record`, since acts are a sparse map of clicked residents rather than an initialized value for every resident.

The manual window trim positions still need care if the upstairs layout changes, and the regularly spaced storefronts still feel orderly for a busy-book street. On the final 240-frame click tour, draw time averaged **2.41 ms**, with **3.48 ms p95** and **298.37 ms worst** when the dusk cache was built. Frame 0 took **499.2 ms** to build the initial town and sprite caches; update averaged **0.01 ms**. The ordinary frames remain comfortably below the 16 ms target. ESLint and all canvas-loop typecheck targets pass.
