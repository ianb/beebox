# alien-bezier — cycle notes

## Cycle 1
**Intent:** First full draft. Every outline is a hand-placed bezier/quad path (head as four beziers, body as a tapered sack, arm tubes as two beziers joined by short lines, legs as quads, ovals from a kappa helper). Flat fills, then bristle strokes clipped inside each shape (dark green along the lower-right rim, light green upper-left highlight), then ink outlines with wobble 0.6 on top. Eyes, cheeks, smile flat/ink on top.
**Seen:** The figure matches the guide's pose and proportion closely; the bezier outlines are smooth and even, with a slight hand wobble. The fills read as flat green at 1x. Zoomed 3x, the bristle texture is there, but the dark rim strokes sit about 10–20px inside the head edge. They form a smudgy band under the cheeks and along the jaw, which reads as dirt more than a shadow under the head's curve. The light-green highlight on the upper left of the head is nearly invisible. The body's rim shading is faint streaks on the right. The cheeks are flat salmon ovals. The ground shadow is fine.

## Cycle 2
**Intent:** Make the shading read as form. Move the head/body rim strokes onto the edge itself (radius 1.0 instead of 0.92, so they are half-clipped and hug the outline), with more and wider strokes. Strengthen and lighten the upper-left head highlight. Add a shadow cast by the head across the top of the body. Replace the flat cheeks with pencil scribbles clipped to an oval.
**Seen:** The head shading now hugs the outline. A darker crescent runs down the right side and along the jaw, so the head reads as rounder than in cycle 1. The upper-left highlight is visible as pale streaky bristle marks (a bit streaky and diagonal, but it helps). The cast shadow under the head shows as a darker band across the top of the body. That works at 1x. The cheeks got worse: the pencil hatch is faint and reads brownish-grey, so the pink blush is gone. The body's rim shading is still weak at 1x.

## Cycle 3
**Intent:** Restore the pink cheeks (a flat pink base at 55% group opacity, with a deeper-pink pencil hatch on top). Give the head and body outlines a pressure envelope that is heavier on the lower, shadow side, for a more hand-inked line. Make the body rim shading stronger (weight 18, opacity 0.6).
**Seen:** The cheeks are pink again. The diagonal pencil hatch shows at 3x zoom, but the 55% pink over green looks slightly dusky/brownish, which is less fresh than the guide's flat pink. I could not see the outline pressure change at 1x. Pixel sampling at x=200 gives a dark run of about 3px at the top of the head in both cycles, and about 3px (cycle 2) vs about 4px (cycle 3) at the bottom, so it is real but very slight. The body's right-side rim shading is a little more visible. Overall the change is small. The parts that remain flat and lifeless are the hands, feet, antenna balls and eye whites.

## Cycle 4
**Intent:** Shade the small round parts (antenna balls in ochre, hands and feet in dark green) with a bristle crescent on the lower right, via a `ball` helper. Add a cool blue-grey lid shadow along the top of each eye white. Make the cheeks a lighter pink at 85% so they stop going dusky. Give the ground shadow a couple of horizontal bristle strokes so it reads as painted rather than as a flat ellipse.
**Seen:** At 1x, the cheeks are now clearly pink and fresher (this helped). The eye whites have a faint blue-grey band along the top that reads as a lid shadow. The ground shadow gained a faint horizontal streak, a small improvement. The small-part crescents mostly failed: zoomed 6x on the raised hand, the bristle "crescent" is two or three faint blotches, and on the antenna balls and feet it is close to invisible. Short bristle marks on 11–24px shapes do not build tone.

## Cycle 5
**Intent:** Replace the failed bristle crescents on small parts (balls, hands, feet) with a flat crescent inside a clip: the shade colour, then the base colour offset up-left, as a group at 55%. Add a few diagonal pencil hatch lines over the lower-right side for texture.
**Seen:** At 1x, the small parts look the same as in cycle 4. Zoomed, the flat crescent is almost hidden. Pixel sampling on the left foot shows darkening only in the last 2–4px inside the outline (e.g. 124,205,106 base → 88,158,78 at x=188,y=448), and the ink line covers most of that. My geometry was wrong: an offset of 0.22·rx / 0.3·ry is only 3–5px on these sizes, and that is about the outline's width. The diagonal pencil hatch on the feet is visible zoomed. To my eye at 4x it looked like lighter streaks, but the samples show it is darker (112,192,97 against 124,205,106), so I misjudged it, probably from contrast with the neighbouring pixels. Net: a small improvement over cycle 4, but not visible at 1x.

## Cycle 6
**Intent:** Make the small-part crescents wide enough to clear the ink line: offset the base-colour oval by 0.38·rx / 0.5·ry, enlarge it 5%, and raise the group opacity to 65%. That should give a visible shaded crescent about 5–8px wide on the lower right of the hands, feet and antenna balls. No other changes, so the effect can be isolated.
**Seen:** This helped. Zoomed, the raised hand and both feet now have a clear darker crescent on the lower right with a soft edge. On the feet it reads as the underside, with the pencil hatch running through it. At 1x it is subtle but visible: the hands and feet look rounded rather than flat, and the antenna balls have a faint ochre lower edge. Nothing else changed, as intended. This is the final frame.
