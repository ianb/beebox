## What the walk achieved

Very little in the box, and that was the finding. The walk spent most of its length
on an upload that would not work, then reached a login wall. It ends with the walker
saying they would give it one more evening *"after my friend fixes the login"*.

## Findings and where they went

The run's largest visible failure — two photographs the composer refused with *"Those
files couldn't be added to the message"* — was **the harness**. An agent preparing the
journey had converted the source photographs to WebP; the boxholder had asked for
ordinary JPEGs. The walk's conclusion, *"maybe this thing only takes JPEGs"*, was a
first-time user's guess about a file we had substituted.

Two real things came out of it anyway:

- The message named neither the format nor the reason, and the only place the format
  appeared was a console line reading `ImageProcessingError`. The walker got there by
  guessing and wrote *"My mother would not have got here."* Fixed in `8cecbe33`.
- The debug log had been destroying every error's message — `Error`'s `message` is
  non-enumerable, so `JSON.stringify` dropped it and the log recorded
  `{"name":"ImageProcessingError"}`. That is why nobody, including the engineer who
  went looking, could say why the upload failed. Fixed in `ab5f483e`.

The WebP rejection itself is filed as noticed-not-scheduled:
[issue](../../../../../issues/bugs/2026-08-23-webp-paste-rejected-though-the-app-emits-webp.md).
The interesting part is not the format but that `canvas-encode.ts` *prefers* WebP for
re-encoded photos, so the app may refuse an image it would itself produce.

The vocabulary the run collected — four words for what the walker could not tell
apart, and `inventory` meaning disk usage — is on
[the vocabulary issue](../../../../../issues/closed/bugs/2026-08-08-implementation-vocab-leaks-into-ui.md).
