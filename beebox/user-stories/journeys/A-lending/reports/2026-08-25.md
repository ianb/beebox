## What the app did well — protect these

The uncertainty handling is the standout of both walks so far, and here it was
tested three ways and held. "Bram (maybe)" survived every turn un-hardened; the
doc entry reads `unknown — possibly lent to Bram, possibly lost` with a
self-instruction — *"this list must never state that Bram has it"* — quoting
the user's own reasoning, and converts the open worry into a dated **decision**
("found it, Bram had it, or buy a new one"). The walker: "the single best thing
in this app."

Also: it retracted its own earlier advice unprompted and rewrote an ask so it
wouldn't read as an accusation; refused to mark a book returned while merely en
route; invented no due date it wasn't given while still scheduling a check-in;
quoted the user's raw words under every row ("in a month I'll be able to see
*why* it says what it says"); answered the payoff question with a short, argued
chase list rather than a todo dump; hedged its own failing timer with a dated
todo *before* being asked, and apologised well; and told the user plainly that
the page they wanted doesn't exist — then offered to build it, which the walker
called "the most interesting sentence anyone has said to me tonight."
