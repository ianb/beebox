An upgrade clinic with two very different kinds of robot eyes. The bearded man with round glasses in his baggy cardigan and a teenage robot in a hoodie face each other across a workbench, both in profile; the robot sits on a stool looking straight at the man, and the man reaches across and presses a pair of huge new camera-lens eyes, each as big as a teacup with concentric rings and a bright highlight, onto the FRONT of the robot's face, where its eyes belong, the way an optician fits glasses; the lenses point the same way the robot's nose and mouth do, toward the man; the robot's old eyes, two tiny dull dots, lie discarded on the bench beside a tray of the big shiny lens eyes. A queue of robots in hoodies waits behind, and every one of them has only the tiny dull dot eyes, two little filled-in dots, blank and sleepy, faces otherwise flat; they stand slack and incurious. On the other side, robots who already have the enormous lens eyes are transformed: wide, shining, ringed eyes taking up most of their faces, and they are stunned by what they now see. One presses its face against a computer screen, one holds a mouse up and examines it like a jewel, one stares at its own hands, one has climbed onto a chair to look at a web page on the wall. Everything the lens-eyed robots look at glows with little radiating lines. The contrast between the tiny dot eyes on the left and the huge lens eyes on the right must be obvious at a glance.
