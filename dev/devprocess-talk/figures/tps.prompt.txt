Use the reference sheet for the robot character and draw in exactly its style: loose, raw 1970s underground outsider comic, black and white pen-and-ink, scratchy quick hatching, wobbly lines, heavy blacks where needed. Pure white paper background, no panel border, no frame. There are no humans in the picture at all; the only figure is the square-headed robot from the sheet.

A dreary office cubicle under a flickering fluorescent tube. A square-headed robot sits hunched at a grey desk, filling in a thick, enormously detailed report, a bound stack of letter-size forms, not a newspaper and not a spread sheet: page after page of small boxes, checkboxes, numbered sections and sub-sections, every single box carefully filled with wobbly scribble, a cover sheet clipped to the front, the pages already filled folded back over the top. A second copy, already stamped, sits in the out-tray. The robot is contentedly busy: a small, quiet, satisfied smile, not a grin, eyes on the page, one finger holding its place, pen in the other hand, a small stack of identical finished reports beside it. On the desk: a stapler, a mug of cold coffee, a wilting potted plant, a wall clock. Above the cubicle wall there is only the flickering tube and the wall clock; nobody is watching.

The report is dense: a grid of dozens of small boxes on every visible page, each box holding a tiny wobbly scribble, headings as scribbled lines. Absolutely no letters or words anywhere in the picture, not on the report, the cover sheet, the mug or the stack: every bit of writing is illegible scribble.
