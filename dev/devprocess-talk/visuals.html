Visual treatments for the supporting slides

Six prototypes, each a 16:9 panel at slide proportions, static SVG, one accent color. Numbers are the verified ones from the inventory. The point of each is stated under it; react to the shape, not the polish.

1. The thesis as one picture. A staircase over the calendar: the riser names the constraint (orange), the tread names what got built (gray). The plateau is visible as a long flat tread. Commit volume per month sits behind it in a tint so the pace reads without a second chart. Reused as a progress marker: on each bottleneck's main slide, the same staircase with that step lit.
2. The motif as small multiples. Three tiny step charts in one row, identical scale logic, one accent. The eye compares shapes, not numbers; the labels carry the only four numbers that matter. The budget is the only green line on the slide, so "the one with a line held" is literally visible.
3. A decision as a flow. Instead of the raw log lines, one source and the outcomes sized by count; the number that proves the rule (zero on unknown) is the only red on the slide and sits in an empty dashed box. The log excerpt can stay on the next supporting slide for the people who want the text.
4. Open vs closed as paired bars. One axis in the middle; what agents can fix lands almost entirely on the right, what needs a human decision splits. The sentence at the bottom is the slide's claim; the shape makes it before you read it.
5. Before and after as mass, not text. The before is deliberately set too small to read: it is a gray wall. The after is set large. Two annotation lines in color say what was removed and what was kept. For the talk, the before can fade in first and then collapse into the after.
6. A count as dots, a breakdown as a strip. Each read is a dot, jittered within its age bucket, so 479 is felt rather than stated; the band tints the part the claim is about. The strip below answers "what for" with lengths, and one quoted exchange at the bottom makes it concrete. The same dot-and-strip shape works for closed issues on the next slide.
